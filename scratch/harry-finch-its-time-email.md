**Subject: Following up — audio documentaries and It's Time**

Hi Harry,

Really good to catch up, and thanks for being open to passing this on to the team.

**My story**
If you want to understand who I am and why I do this, there's a short video on the website that says it better than I can in an email: [meandmyoldman.co.uk](https://www.meandmyoldman.co.uk). My dad got dementia, and I never managed to properly capture his stories before he passed. He died the same week my son was born.

**Quick version of what I do**
I sit down with families and record long-form conversations with the people who knew someone best, their children, siblings, or oldest friends, then turn it into an audio documentary of that person's life. Not an interview. A story, told in their own words and the words of the people who loved them.

Most of what I do is for families while a parent is still alive. But I've also done this for people who've already lost someone, working from the memories of everyone who knew them. I worked on a beautiful project for a woman who lost her husband to a brain tumour; she posthumously had his children after fighting in the Scottish courts. I helped capture her husband's life story through the voices of those who knew him best, for the children he would never meet. It's a project close to my heart, and one of the things that triggered me to do the same for my dad. You can hear what it meant to her here: [stories of those we've lost](https://www.meandmyoldman.co.uk/#stories-of-those-weve-lost).

That's the whole reason this exists, and it's also why It's Time and a dementia charity I'm talking to are the two I want to support properly, not just as a line on a website.

**Three things I mentioned, in case it's useful to have them in writing**

**1. Referral gift.** Any family who refers another family gets a free one-off recording, a snapshot of their own life story now. If It's Time sends someone my way, I'd like to donate £50 to the charity for every referral, and I'm happy to name It's Time as one of the charities I support, alongside the dementia charity. No obligation beyond letting me use your name. Here's the referral page so you can see how it works: [meandmyoldman.co.uk/refer](https://www.meandmyoldman.co.uk/refer).

**2. Content collaboration.** This is Freddie, not Oscar, sorry, I had my names muddled on the call. His Instagram is [@f_oscar_r](https://www.instagram.com/f_oscar_r/). He's lost both his parents and is looking for content. It's Time is looking for reach. I think the whole thing could be built around his experience, me interviewing the people who knew his parents, him telling the story of what that uncovered, with him front and centre and It's Time behind it.

**3. A service for your community.** This is the bigger question, and the one I'd most want your clinical leads' view on: could capturing the life story of someone's parent, from the people who knew them, be a positive part of what It's Time offers? Separately, I'd be really interested to know if there's any research your clinical team is aware of on the impact of an exercise like this, being interviewed about someone you've lost, or hearing others talk about them, on the grief journey. I'd want proper guardrails and your team's judgement before anything moved further either way.

Happy to jump on a call with whoever makes sense on your end, whenever it's useful.

Thanks again, Harry.
