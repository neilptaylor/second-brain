The Front Row: Hormozi review v2

8 October 2026. This replaces v1, which only used YouTube.

Sources used: $100M Offers, the $100M Leads guide, the Lead Nurture playbook, the hormozi-techniques proposal checklist, and his YouTube teaching from January to October 2026 (Hormozi speaking in every point, no guests). Not used: $100M Money Models. It isn't on this machine, and it's the book with the most on pricing and getting cash in early, so send it over and I'll recheck the fix.

Score: 4 out of 10

The books mark it lower than YouTube did. Run against the Grand Slam Offer checklist, the Front Row has a price and a reason why, and very little else.

What works

  • Reason why. "You're first in, you help me build it." Offers says every offer needs one, or a low price reads as suspicious.
  • Honest scarcity. Ten places is real: each one is about 3.5 hours of your time a year. Offers calls honest capacity the most ethical scarcity there is.
  • The yearly session. On YouTube he says the real prize of a cheap membership is the guaranteed annual appointment where you can sell more (29 Aug 2026, 13:10).

What loses marks

  • It discounts the core offer. Offers is blunt about this: never discount the main thing, it teaches people your prices are negotiable. Add bonuses instead. The Front Row is the main Snapshot at a third of the price.
  • The discount doesn't buy anything specific. Leads allows a lower price for changed terms. Their example: you can have it cheaper if you introduce me to three friends right now. "Share it once with your people" is too vague to count as changed terms.
  • No value stack. Nothing has a price tag, so £99 has nothing to look small next to.
  • No guarantee. Every proposal you send carries one. The Front Row doesn't.
  • No bonuses, and no deadline. The checklist wants bonuses that cost little, each with a price tag and an expiry date.
  • Paid yearly. Five chances to walk away. On YouTube he says a prepaid customer can't churn, and the cash arrives now (9 Jan 2026, 47:59).
  • The friend link is a second discount. At £249, a friend leaves you £54. Leads says pay your cost to get a customer, split between the referrer and the friend, so they both win. The £50 is right. The cut price for the friend isn't.

The fix

1. Change the terms, not just the price. The five-year price is £1,095. Front Row members pay £495, all up front, because they give you three things: a two-minute video after their first Snapshot, a personal introduction to three people (a text or an email that names you, not a post), and their name on the page. That's a different deal, not a discount. Leads backs it, and the line for anyone who asks is simple: "They got it for less because they introduced me to three people. Happy to do the same for you."

2. Stack the value, with a price on everything.

  • Five Snapshots, one a year. Anchor £750 each (pricing.md): £3,750
  • The video every year, normally £50 on top: £250
  • The reflection form, so you don't arrive with "erm, work's been busy": [your price]
  • The camera briefing, so you forget the camera's there: [your price]
  • Delivered on your birthday: [your price]
  • Price to the Front Row: £495

The last three already exist or cost nothing, which is what the checklist means by a good bonus. You set their prices. I haven't invented them.

3. Add a guarantee. If your first Snapshot doesn't feel worth the money, I refund all £495 and you keep it. You lose at most £170 of your time per refund, and you've never had a refund claimed on the family documentary guarantee.

4. Add a real deadline. Offers' favourite is a rolling cohort: the Front Row records together in one month, and if you miss it you wait for the next round. Pick the month and the closing date.

5. Referral that pays both sides. The friend pays the full £299 and gets the video free (worth £50). The Front Row member gets £50. Ask for names on the day they pay, not a month later. Leads' line, put into your words: "Who would you want to do this with?" A partner or a sibling is the obvious answer.

6. Yearly payment as the fallback only. £149 a year, offered only to someone who says no to paying up front.

7. Reply to every application the same day, yourself. The Lead Nurture playbook says speed to contact and personal follow-up decide who turns up. Once someone's in, set automatic reminders before each yearly session and tell them they're automatic.

8. The family documentary question, every year. Ask it in the last ten minutes of every session. One Hero Story at £2,400, at the bottom of the 60% to 66% margin band, leaves about £1,440. That covers four members' share of the cost on its own.

9. The name. Keep The Front Row, and give it a line underneath that says what they get and over how long, as Offers' naming chapter suggests. For example: "Five years of your story, one year at a time."

With the fix: 8.5 out of 10

The Snapshots on their own still cost you more than £495 (£850 of your time over five years). It works because the price now buys three introductions, not a vague share, the cash comes in on day one, a guarantee takes away the risk, and every yearly session leads to a family documentary. The missing 1.5: no case study yet (the Founding Five will give you one), and the decade bonus is still parked.
