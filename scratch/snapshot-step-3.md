## 1. Positioning, v2

**One breath:** Snapshot is the recording my families' children wish existed: you, at this age, telling your story while you're still living it.

**The paragraph:** I've spent 350 hours recording parents in their seventies and eighties, for children who wish they'd asked sooner. What those hours taught me is how much of the middle goes. The years with small children, the decision made at 2am, the drive home from the school gate on the first day. People remember that it happened. The feeling went years ago. Snapshot is the same work, done thirty years earlier. An hour and a half with me, about your life as it is now, handed back within a week as edited audio and short clips. For you, and one day for your children, who'll want to know who you were when they were small. Not coaching, not therapy. Just space, and your stories, while you can still tell them first-hand.

### Hormozi's view

From the YouTube corpus, 2026 teaching.

- **Your price worry is a comparison problem, not a price problem.** "Customers default to price when you give them no other variables" (26 Jun 2026). Put it next to therapy and coaching and it loses: same hour, less expertise, nothing solved. So never let it be a session. The buyer is paying for a finished recording of their life at 40, which neither therapy nor coaching gives them. Change the comparison from apples to oranges.
- **It's an attraction offer. Its job is to find families, not make profit.** His $10 bootcamp roughly breaks even and the membership behind it is the profit (10 Jul 2026). A small paid step also filters people and gets a card on file (7 Aug 2026). Small jobs chunk up into big ones (29 Aug 2026).
- **Put the thing clients love most on the front end** (30 Jan 2026). For you that's the listening. One family said nobody had ever given their dad "so much attention without anyone interrupting him". Snapshot is that, sold on its own.
- **Name the dream outcome, not the feature.** "Space and stories" describes the feature. The outcome is your kids hearing you at 40. The positioning now leads with that.

What to ignore: countdown timers and giveaways. "Small batches" is real scarcity, and that's enough for this audience.

### Gray's view

The brain has no Matt Gray corpus, so this comes from his public teaching. Weigh it more lightly than the Hormozi points.

- **The product should grow out of the founder story.** Yours does: dad, the voice you can't recall, 350 hours, "maybe this is all a bit late". Lead with the realisation and the offer explains itself. That's why v2 opens on the 350 hours.
- **One idea per piece.** The idea is "too late". Everything else backs it up.
- **The reader is the hero and you're the guide.** Keep your story to two lines on the page. The rest is about them.
- **Specific beats clever.** The school gate, 2am, the seven-year-old. Keep them.

## 2. Pricing, v2: making it viable

The 4x option is gone.

You're right that £300 for "a session" loses to coaching. Three things fix that together:

**a) Sell a finished recording, not a session.** The comparison should be a family photoshoot, not a therapist. People pay a photographer for how they looked this year, and Snapshot is how they sounded and what they thought. Cheap production lifts that fit inside your hour of editing:
- open and close with the same music as the family documentaries (check the licence covers it)
- a ten-second spoken intro from you with their name and the date, so it plays like a time capsule
- a titled cover card, and a private listening link rather than a raw file

**b) Make it the way into the main offer.** The Snapshot price comes off any family documentary booked within 12 months. On the call, ask "and your mum and dad?" in your own words. People in their forties are the people who buy Hero Story.

**c) Price it as a way in, and cap it.** Recommended launch numbers:

| | Price | Cost | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Snapshot, 1.5 hrs | **£195** | £145 | 25.6% |
| Two a year, 1.5 hrs now plus 1 hr six months on | **£350** | £250 | 28.6% |

Both are under the 50% floor, and that's deliberate. It's the cost of finding families, paid in your hours rather than cash, since the cash cost is nearly nil. Selling at £195 instead of the £290 floor gives up £95 of your time per Snapshot. One Hero Story at £2,400 and roughly 60% margin is about £1,450. So **one conversion covers roughly fifteen Snapshots.** Cap it at 4 a month so it can't eat into main-offer time.

Check before locking: a full £195 credit on a Hero Story lands 8.1% off LIST, £15 past the 7.5% floor. Either cap the credit at £180, or accept it because the Snapshot has already paid £195.

The alternative, standalone at the 50% floor: £300 one-off, £500 for two a year (second session 1 hr). Only worth it if (a) lifts it clearly above a coaching hour.
