## Before the call

- Book it at the end of their Snapshot session, while they're still glowing.
- Send the Riverside link. Ask them to be somewhere quiet with their face in good light.

## Part 1: the four questions (15 minutes)

**One ask up front:** "Could you start each answer with the question in it? So you'd begin: before we started, I thought…" That way every answer works on its own as a clip.

1. **What you expected:** "Before we started, what did you think this was going to be?"
2. **What happened:** "And what actually happened? Was there a moment it stopped feeling like an interview?"
3. **The deepest impact:** "What's stayed with you since? Anything you've caught yourself thinking about, or said to someone at home?"
4. **To someone considering it:** "If a friend was on the fence about doing one, what would you say to them?"

Let the silences run. The best line usually comes after the first answer, when they add "…actually, the thing is…"

Close part 1: "Are you happy for me to use short clips and a line or two from this to show others what Snapshot is like?" Get a clear yes on the recording.

## Part 2: what it's worth (10 minutes)

The problem: they love you, so they'll be kind. Kind means a number that's either too high (to flatter you) or too low (to protect you). So don't ask "what would you pay?" straight. Ask what a stranger would think, and ask about behaviour, not opinion.

Do this before you tell them the offer. Once they've heard £199, every answer bends towards it.

**The permission line, said once:**

> "Right, the bit I actually need your help with. I've no idea what to charge for this, and you're the only people who've done it. So the kindest thing you can do is give me the honest number, not the nice one. If you think it's worth fifty quid, tell me fifty quid."

**The four price questions, in this order:**

1. **Too cheap.** "Imagine a friend sent you the link and you'd never met me. At what price would you think, that's so cheap it can't be any good?"
2. **A bargain.** "What price would feel like a proper bargain, a no-brainer?"
3. **Getting pricey.** "At what price would you start to think, it's good, but I'd need to think about it?"
4. **Too much.** "And at what price would you say, lovely idea, not for me?"

Write down all four numbers. The sweet spot sits between their "bargain" and their "getting pricey".

**Then two questions about behaviour:**

5. **Next year.** "If I said I'd do this with you every year, the week before your birthday, would you want that? What would make it a yes, and what would make it a no?"
6. **Who else.** "Is there anyone you'd buy one for? Your other half, a parent, a friend?"

Question 6 tells you whether the partner idea and gifting are real. Don't suggest names. Let them come up with them.

## Part 3: the founders' offer (5 minutes)

Now tell them. This is the real test. Everything in part 2 is opinion. A yes or no here is the only number that counts.

> "Thank you, that's brilliant. So here's what I'd like to offer you, because you were first. If you want to do this every year, you can have it for £199 a year, and that price is yours for five years, whatever I end up charging everyone else. And for every friend who books one, you get a year free. It comes off your next renewal."

Then stop talking. Let them answer.

- **If yes:** "Brilliant. I'll send you the details. Your next one's the week before your birthday." Note their birthday.
- **If "maybe":** "No rush at all. Can I ask what's making you think about it?" That answer is worth more than the yes.
- **If no:** "Totally fine. What would have made it a yes?"

Never apologise for the price. Never drop it on the call.

## After the call

- Send the video version of their Snapshot. That's their founding thank you.
- Pull one 60 to 90 second clip from question 2 or 3.
- Pull one written line, usually from question 4.
- Log their four price numbers, their two behaviour answers and their yes, maybe or no in one sheet. Six rows. Look at them together after the last call, not one at a time.
- Note anything that nearly stopped them from booking. That's copy for the landing page.

## Reading the six

- If most say yes at £199, the price is probably too low. Try £249 on the next wave.
- If most say maybe, listen to why. It's usually the yearly commitment, not the number.
- If "too cheap" answers sit above £99, that confirms the £99 pilot was a gift, not a price.
- Emma is your sister. Count her numbers, but weigh them lighter.
