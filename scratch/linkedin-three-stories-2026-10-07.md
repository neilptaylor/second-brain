## 1. Two Pages On The House (Pain Points, Lead-gen, CTA: Save)

```
A man told me his dad once tried to write his own life story.

He got two pages in.

Two pages on why he named the house what he did.
Then nothing from the day his son was born
until the day his son left for university.

Those were the years the son was in the room for.

I think that's the pattern.
We assume we already know the middle, so nobody tells it, nobody asks.

Most parents have three kinds of story:

Where they started.
What changed them.
And the one they could still talk about at 2am if nobody was timing them.

We usually only get the first. It's the safest, and it's been polished by years of telling.

So next call, skip "how are you?" and try this:

"What could you talk about for hours?"

Then stop talking.

Save this for your next call.
```

Hook alternatives:
- "Two pages on why he named the house. Then nothing for eighteen years."
- "The years we were in the room for are the ones nobody tells us about."

Swap for Neil: confirm the man is happy to be used anonymously (it comes from a prospect call). "Eighteen years" in the second hook is my arithmetic from "birth to university", change if wrong.

## 2. Stories, Not Skeletons (The Now, Reach, CTA: Repost)

```
A woman told me her dad lost his father when he was sixteen.

He has never told the family the full story.
She has pieced it together over the years.
A date here. A sentence there.

She'd love to ask.
She also knows what it costs him when she gets near it.

Not every story is ready.

Some are scars. He can show you, and talk about them.
Some are still wounds, and you don't start there.

So start in the room next door.
What the house smelt like.
What he did on Saturdays.
Who he sat next to at school.

The hard room might open on its own.
Or it might stay ajar, and that's his call.

That's what I mean by stories, not skeletons.

Repost if there's a door in your family you've been standing outside.
```

Hook alternatives:
- "She'd love to ask her dad about it. She also knows what it costs him."
- "Some stories are scars. Some are still wounds. You don't start with the wound."

Swap for Neil: she described her dad as hard to open up on emotional things. I kept it to the one fact (father died at sixteen, never told in full). Check she's fine with being used anonymously.

## 3. The Story I Didn't Ask For (The Drift, Trust, CTA: Repost)

```
I have the stories my dad chose to tell.

I don't have the ones he'd have told if I'd asked.

He died in 2021. My son was born six days later.

Most days now I can't hear his voice properly.
It's fading, a bit more each year.

My son is five.
He'll get a man called Patrick from me.
Secondhand.
Edited by my memory.

The story a parent would tell at 2am never gets volunteered.
Someone has to ask.

Some of us still can.

Repost if there's someone you can still ask.
```

Hook alternatives:
- "My son is five. He'll only ever know my dad secondhand."
- "I can't hear my dad's voice properly any more."

Swap for Neil: one real thing Patrick did tell you, a specific story, in place of "the stories my dad chose to tell". That's the soul of this one.
