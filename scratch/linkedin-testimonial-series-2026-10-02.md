## 1. Colin, "I didn't recognise my voice" | Fri 2 Oct (today)

Image: MMOM_testimonial_01_LinkedIn-Instagram_1080x1350.png
https://drive.google.com/file/d/1z-00-qh7I0z4f_5bsg6erZmXnDxTlizK/view

```
Yesterday was a tough Thursday.

Nothing dramatic.
Just one of those days where my own head was the problem.
Doubting the work.
Doubting whether any of it really reaches people.

Then this arrived from a dad this morning.

He'd listened to every chapter of his audio documentary.
And wants to listen to it all again.
Maybe starting tonight.

"I didn't recognise my voice. It, like me, has aged. It used to sound very youthful!"

(None of us sound the way we think we do. Ask anyone who's heard their own voicemail.)

But this is the line I keep going back to:

"At the moment it feels like I haven't talked enough about those years when we were a family of four."

Hours of talking.
And his first feeling is that there's more.

That's not a gap in the recording.
That's a door he's just found in his own house.

Then the bit that made my week:

"What happens now? I'm going to try to talk to my kids before the family round up."

A dad, picking up the phone to his grown-up children.
To talk about the years they were all under one roof.

That's the whole point.
Not the audio.
The phone call it starts.

Thank you. You turned my Friday around.

Repost if there's a dad in your life with more to say than he lets on.
```

Alt hook A: `"I didn't recognise my voice. It, like me, has aged."` / `A dad sent me that this morning, after listening to his own life story.`
Alt hook B: `I nearly didn't post today.` / `Then a dad sent me a message that turned the whole week around.`

## 4. Judy, "hear what my kids had to say" | Wed 4 Nov

Image: MMOM_testimonial_09_LinkedIn_1200x627.png
https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Iy6M-61H0ngzdz7MtERdEfJRlZCo47os/view

```
I asked a mum if I could share a clip from her sessions.

She said yes.

"I'd be comfortable even if it shows me rambling on or correcting my sentences!"

Those are usually my favourite bits.

But it was the line after that I keep coming back to:

"I am so thankful that I was able to do that with you and hear what my kids had to say."

Before I ever interview a parent, I sit down with each of their children.
An hour each.
What they remember.
What they've always wondered.
What they've never quite said.

Then the parent hears it.

Most parents never hear what their grown-up children would say about them.

Not because it isn't there.
Because there's never a moment where it gets said.

She put it better than I can:

"The entire process certainly stirs one's emotions and makes us think of our lives with a parent or a child in a different way."

Repost if there's something you've never quite said to your mum.
```
