# Kevin Maguire: briefing for 13 October

The New Fatherhood. Book out this year (Balance), weekly Substack at thenewfatherhood.org that became the book, a podcast that's gone quiet, Instagram @newfatherhood. Lives in Barcelona. Ex-Google, has worked with Amazon, Netflix, Daniel Pink and Oliver Burkeman (your notes to Steve, 7 Oct).

## Open with this

The chapter on noticing. He gets asked for the one piece of advice for new parents, and his answer is:

> "Buy a small notebook, keep it handy, and write down things you notice about them."

Then, straight after: "I didn't do it. I wish I had." His newsletter became the only record of his kids' early years that stuck.

How to land it: "The bit that got me was the notebook. And then you admitting you never did it. That's my whole job, basically. I'm the notebook people didn't keep."

Backup line, same page: "'Future You' will one day offer thanks to 'Today You'." That is the Snapshot pitch in his own words.

## Things to know about him (all from the book)

- His dad drove a Ford Transit up and down the UK laying cable, paid by the mile. Parents are Irish. Church every Sunday.
- Paternal postpartum depression after his son was born. Says he doesn't recognise himself in photos from then. The first mate he told knew two other dads who'd been through it. "In private, men would tell me. In public, crickets."
- Kids are Padme and Bodhi. His mum passed away about four years ago, a few months after he started boxing with three mates ("Team Daddy"). Last week's newsletter was about it.
- Agents and publishers told him "Dads don't read!" He pointed at the thousands reading the newsletter every week.
- About 297 paying supporters keep the newsletter free. He's running a reader survey right now: 1 in 8 readers were women last time.
- "Noticing in public" is how he describes the newsletter. His favourite writing book is Several Short Sentences About Writing by Verlyn Klinkenborg.

- **The last time.** "There will be a time when you pick your child up for the final time, and you won't even realise it." He knows the exact date he last lifted his daughter into the top bunk: 26 April 2023. The night he put his back out.

## The offer: keep it loose, no numbers

Pricing is parked, so tomorrow has one number in it: free. No £199, no £50, no link. If he asks what it costs other people, say "I'm still working that out with the first few. That's partly why I'd love you to try it."

Hold it for the last ten minutes. Only if the chat has been warm. If it hasn't, skip it and send it by email later.

### The script

**1. Describe it, in his language.**

> "Can I tell you about something I've just started? It's called Snapshot. It's your notebook, basically. An hour and a half with me, online, about the year you've just lived. The kids, the work, the bits you'd forget by Christmas. Back within 7 days as about an hour of audio, the story of your year, in your own voice."

**2. Ask what he'd pay. Then stop talking.**

> "Out of interest, if one of your readers sent you that, what would you expect it to cost?"

Write his number down. It's real data, from someone who prices his own work.

**3. Give it to him.**

> "I'd love to give you one. On me. No strings."

**4. Say what happens next, up front, both ways.**

> "Here's the honest bit. If you love it, I'd like to have a chat about whether it's right for your readers. If it's not for you, or not for them, tell me why. That's just as useful to me, and you won't hurt my feelings. Either way you get the story of your year."

That's the whole ask. He knows where it might go, and he knows no is fine.

**5. Book it before you hang up.** Pick a date in the next two weeks, while it's warm. Send him the pre-session form the same day.

### If he does it but doesn't love it

Ask him, on a separate short call, what was missing. A man who writes about noticing for a living will tell you exactly. That's a free edit of your product from a sharp writer. Worth more than £145 of your time.

### If he loves it but can't promote it

Don't push. Some newsletters don't take anything commercial, and he's built trust with 297 paying supporters by being careful. Ask: "Is there a way you'd be comfortable mentioning it, or is that a no?" Options he might prefer, in order:

1. He writes about the experience himself, in his own words, no link. Noticing in public, which is his whole thing.
2. A single line in a roundup or a PS.
3. One introduction to someone he thinks would love it.

If all three are no, thank him and leave it. You've still got a testimonial and a friend.

### Later, not tomorrow

When he's heard his and wants to share it: his readers get the video version included, and he gets a fee for every booking through his link. Numbers decided once pricing is settled.

**The quiet door.** If he talks about his own dad, don't pitch. Note it. Family documentary conversation later, in your own time.
