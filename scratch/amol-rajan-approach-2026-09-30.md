# Amol Rajan: how to get on his radar

Status: draft, scratch. Nothing here has been sent.

## The honest read

There is no single message that does it. He has 10,000 members and a small team ("the Supreme Council of ARC"). What gets through is being a familiar, useful name over six to eight weeks, then one well-timed ask. You are not low fry here, you are the person whose job is the thing he said he wants to happen.

## Your crossovers, in his own words from this newsletter

1. "I want people of my generation and older to record their parents' Desert Island Discs while they still have the chance." That is your work, almost word for word.
2. "Ask yourself: who would you organise a Celebration for?" Your families tell their dad how proud they are while he is alive to hear it.
3. His dad died, and he never heard the family say what he meant to them in one place. Your dad died, your son was born six days later.
4. He writes to his children and keeps email addresses for them until they are 18. Same instinct as yours.
5. He officiates memorials and hosted the Letters to Our Sons night. Mortality, fathers, sons is his territory.

The Desert Island Discs line is your door. Lead with it everywhere.

## What you can ask him for (in order of how easy it is to get)

1. A reply. Any reply.
2. A quote of something you wrote in his newsletter or on his posts. He quotes readers (Alex Greenland, Heta Fell, Pippa Probert this week). This is the cheapest "mention" you will get.
3. Your question answered in the members-only Ask Me Anything or the Monday Q+A.
4. A conversation (call, coffee, or a meet and greet slot).
5. A feature: his parents' Desert Island Discs recorded by you, or you on the show. Not before step 4. Do not ask for this in month one.

**Weeks 5 to 8**
- Only now, if he has replied once or quoted you: ask for twenty minutes. Offer a concrete gift, for example recording a session with one of his parents as a thank you. Margin-check that against Cost Model V10 first, and only offer it if you would be glad to do it.
