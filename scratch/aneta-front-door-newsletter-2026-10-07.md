Subject: A bath with a door on it
Preview: Her children had never heard it.

Hey,

There's one question I ask every parent before anything else.

"Think of the house you lived in when you were eight. Stand outside the front door. Now walk me through it, using all five senses."

One woman got as far as the blue front door and the roses beside it, and stopped.

She said, "Actually, I think that might be my first memory. The smell of roses."

Her children were in the next room, getting ready for their part of the interview. None of them knew.

Another woman took me into her family's kitchen in the 1950s. There was a bath in it, and I thought I'd misheard.

They put a door over the bath and ate their dinner on it. Then the door came off and they bathed in the kitchen, because it was the warmest room in the house.

I was born in the eighties. I'd never have thought to ask about that. Her children hadn't heard it either.

Most of us ask our parents the same few things.
How's the knee.
Did you see the match.
I once spent a whole twelve-minute call with my mum on the dishwasher.

The answers to the better questions are there. We just stay in the lanes we know, because we think we've heard it all.

So next time you're with them, try this. Ask them to stand outside the front door of the house they lived in at eight, and walk you in. Then let them go slowly.

If you'd like more questions that open things up like that, I put the best ones into a short guide: [The Conversation Playbook](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1afjgkZ8f9BgbOhdJxR76v9OOxp-idaw8aLxoUnhTdm8/edit?usp=sharing)

Neil

P.S. I'd give a lot to hear my dad walk me through his front door at eight.
