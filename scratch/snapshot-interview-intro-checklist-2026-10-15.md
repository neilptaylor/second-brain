# Snapshot Interview Intro Checklist

Last updated 20261015. Built from the parent checklist, the Emma Fletcher session (14 Oct 2026) and Neil's session notes.

## Housekeeping

- Wired headphones
- Quiet room (soft walls and carpets if possible)
- Phone off
- Computer charging
- Glass of water
- Any ticking in your room?
- Internet checked before we start (Emma lost hers on the Monday)
- Make the video window half the screen, tucked in the corner, so you look at the camera and not at yourself
- Have your form answers in front of you (sent the day before, read through before we start)

## The Plan

- Around 90 minutes, semi-structured, I'll prompt you and guide you, so all you need to do is talk
- You will do most of the talking
- This is private and intimate, it's your space
- I won't discuss what you say with anyone outside this call
- I'm listening deeply. I'm not a coach and I'm not a therapist
- We're capturing a season of your life, not a stage
- If I'm looking down, I'm listening very intently and writing notes
- I'll put myself on mute for most of it, so there may be a delay
- I'll nod and laugh and give you non-verbal cues, but I won't interrupt
- Respond with a full sentence, so your answer still makes sense when you listen back on your own
- There's no right or wrong and no performance. Whatever comes out is the snapshot

## Opening

- 5 second countdown
- "Hi, this is [name], recording my first snapshot, season one, [date]" (say it again if it flops, we pick the best take)
- "What's today's date?"
- "Talk me through your day so far, from the moment you woke up to where you're sitting now, and finish by telling me exactly where you are right now"

## Flow

1. Why are you doing this? Who's it for?
2. Take us back to the start of this season of your life
3. A story to dig into. Offer three choices from their form and let them pick one, and give them the first words of the sentence
4. The people in their corner
5. The photo of the year. What it captures. Follow up: what changes have you seen in the kids (or the people in it)?
6. High point
7. Hard point, and what they learnt from it
8. What surprised them
9. Headline for the year
10. A phrase they've said a lot this year
11. The next 12 months, the next season
12. In 10 years, what do you want to hear when you listen back?
13. Anything else?
14. Quickfire round (five short ones, see below)
15. Sign off: "And that's a wrap, season one." Tag at the start and the end

Ask "how do you feel right now?" as a sentence starter whenever the answer stays on facts.

## Quickfire (shortlist, 20261015)

Things that date the year
1. What was the song you couldn't stop playing?
2. What was the one thing you googled more than anything else?
3. What was the last thing you binge-watched?
4. What was the app or website you spent too much time on?
5. What did you spend money on that you'd now rather not admit?
6. What was the news story everyone kept talking about?

Things to laugh at later
7. What was your biggest "what was I thinking?" moment?
8. What was the most embarrassing thing that happened to you?
9. What was the best bad idea you had?
10. What was the argument you now find funny?
11. What was the most stupid thing you worried about?
12. What did you lose, break or forget, and where did it turn up?
13. What was the funniest thing someone else said or did near you?
14. Who made you laugh the most?

Mark the year out of 10
15. Give the year a score for sleep, for fun and for stress.
16. Give yourself a score as a cook, as a driver and as a friend.
17. Give the year a title for a sitcom episode.

Predictions to check next year
18. What's your prediction for next year, in one line?
19. What do you think you'll be laughing about by next year?
20. What do you think you'll still be worrying about?
21. What's a bet you'd make with yourself for the next 12 months?

Small and specific
22. What was your go-to takeaway or snack?
23. What was the moment you were most proud of yourself, however small?
24. What happened this year that you'd love to see on a replay?

## After the call

- Ask how the style felt, and how the experience felt
- Book the next call before we hang up (feedback, a week later)
- Edit: pull the photo of the year segment (about 56 seconds), cut the technical chatter (Emma: 1:18 to 1:22), mark track segments
- Send her the first read within 24 hours
- Ask them to message me how it feels as they go about their day, and again when they listen back

## Open questions for Neil

- The "family member you wish you'd asked" question felt shoehorned, so it's left out of the flow. Emma suggested: "Has this made you want to know how someone in your life felt at this point in their life?"
