## 1. Losing his father, aged 17
**Source: S2**

Fully clean Vaughan monologue — Neil doesn't speak at all across this whole span, so no cuts needed. Runs about 2:08, a touch over the 2 min ceiling; trim note below if you need it tighter.

**Clip: 40:41.922 – 42:50.318 (Vaughan)**

> "Well, I think it must have been a head injury. Because what happened in May 73 was that he was hospitalised with a stroke. And so I know the family had to, my mother in particular, had to face the difficult decision of cutting off his life support system. But I don't think we children were involved in that. So that may have been grandparents and mother in particular. I don't remember any hospital visits either, but in terms of the major decision to switch off life support, I don't remember having any involvement in that. [tape starts mid-sentence] ...I kind of feel slightly guilty at not actually being a member of it, any of it. And I was undoubtedly wrapped up in A-level preparation or whatever at the time. Somehow, my mother just kept us going, or kept me going, getting on with the job in hand. And as I say, I don't remember very much about this at all. I don't remember anybody talking to me about it. I don't remember getting any special treatment at school about it or anything like that. It's just that he'd been there, I knew he'd been ill and now he wasn't there."

- **Cleanest possible ending:** cut on "...now he wasn't there" (42:50.318) — don't run into the next line ("Now I don't remember," 43:09.548), which is a separate, flatter beat.

## 2. Facing his own mortality, and his mother's death
**Source: S3**

This is one continuous reflection triggered by Neil asking about the triple heart bypass and death. Two sub-moments, both strong. Recommending the second as the primary clip (more universal, stronger payoff); the first works as an optional lead-in.

**Optional lead-in — the trolley/surgery moment: 02:10:10.959 – 02:11:51.156 (Vaughan, no Neil at all, ~1:40, self-contained on its own)**

> "So being in Harefield and having a postponement obviously adds a sense of anxiety to something you've prepared yourself for and then you've prepared yourself again, and anyway the operation got done on the Friday afternoon, and you sometimes think if you're going to choose a time in the week perhaps Friday afternoon isn't it. But I remember being on the trolley before the anaesthetic, before the operation, and kind of having a conversation with God along the lines of, well, I'm kind of trusting that this is going to solve the problems of my health, in which case I will commit myself to living positively afterwards. But if it's not, then I accept that the other is, you know, I will then discover whether the Christian faith works in death or not. That's where I'm at. I can't remember much after that, they do the anaesthetic thing. Sue, unfortunately, visited me when I was in the ICU, when apparently I looked absolutely awful and she got quite upset. Jeff and Nicola might have visited as well before I came around. Then I just puked for ages and felt really dreadful, could hardly move. Six months later, I was walking four and a half, five miles without any problem and back at work."

**Recommended main clip: 02:13:08.191 – 02:14:48.712 (Vaughan, ~1:40)**

> "The thought is that this is going to happen to all of us and it's the big unknown. You don't know when it will come, you don't know how you will cope with it, and you don't know what's the other side of the curtain, if there is anything the other side of the curtain. I mean, Christian faith says there is, but you only find that out in faith after the event. Since the rest of Christianity works for me, then I'm happy to hold on to that. But the third factor in this is that my mother used to say, said it very often in her later years, that she was not frightened of death — which I sort of, it's quite interesting, and I think matches the way she lived. Because she was always positive and friendly. And although the way she died was awful, because she had dementia and took a long time to go when we knew she was going, she had lived her life positively. And remembering that she said she didn't fear death, I believe that was what she felt. And I kind of feel something of the same. And it certainly isn't going to... I accept I'm going to slow down and I accept I'm going to die at some point, but it isn't going to stop me living positively in between as best I can."

- The full 02:12:19.241–02:14:48.712 stretch (which includes the Julian Barnes setup — "he thinks about death every day") is also usable if you want more run-up before the payoff, at the cost of pushing past 2 min once the two Neil cuts are made.
- Strong closing line either way: "...it isn't going to stop me living positively in between as best I can."
