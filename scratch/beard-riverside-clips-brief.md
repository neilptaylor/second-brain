## Stories

### 1. The rabbit for Sunday dinner (Richard, Ch2, 17:12 to 19:04)
**Summary:** Richard, aged six or seven, goes rabbit hunting with his dad on a Saturday night. No rabbit meant no meat on Sunday. Reflective, rationing era.

> "I remember very often Saturday my father would say, "Come on, Richard, let's go and see if we can get a rabbit for tomorrow." So on Saturday evening, we'd go across the fields... we'd stalk the rabbits, we'd crawl on our bellies and we'd get to within about fifty feet. My father would get the catapult and go for a rabbit and he was actually a really dead shot, it'd invariably get them right between the eyes... If we were unsuccessful in getting a rabbit on a Saturday evening, we didn't eat meat on Sunday."

### 2. The 1947 flood: "We're British. We don't panic" (Richard, Ch2, 39:22 to 42:45)
**Summary:** Richard's first memory, aged two. The Avon floods the cottage and the Army hands milk and bread through the bedroom window. His father's rule for life.

> "My memory is of the British Army coming around supplying milk and bread from flat bottom boats and handing it to us through the bedroom windows because the water was that far up... One of my father's sayings to me was, "Just remember we're British. The only thing we don't do is panic." ...You don't get out of your pram and start getting hysterical."

### 3. Seven brothers and the bulldozer at Belsen (Richard, Ch2, 42:52 to 47:21)
**Summary:** The day after war was declared, seven brothers walked four miles to volunteer. Six went. Two died at Dunkirk, two in Burma. One uncle drove the bulldozer in the Belsen newsreel and never spoke of it. The war story Daniel said he had never heard. Poignant.

> "On the fourth of September, the seven brothers, my father being one of them, walked four miles to the next village... They all volunteered for the British Army... my father was the one that was left and the other six went into the army... Two were killed at Dunkirk, two were killed in Burma by the Japanese... there's a very famous BBC newsreel of the British Army liberating the concentration camp at Belsen... The guy driving the bulldozer was one of my uncles. He would never talk, he would never talk about it."

### 4. Standing room only (Richard, Ch2, 1:02:14 to 1:04:17)
**Summary:** Richard on his father George: allotments, twelve-hour days, cutting old men's hair on a Sunday. Ends with his funeral, people standing on boxes to see in through the church windows. Richard is in tears. Strongest poignant clip in the set.

> "He saw himself as an ordinary plain working guy and his role in life was to work and provide for his family. He was very well regarded by people as someone who you could always go to for help... When he died, at his funeral in the church, there was standing room only. And there were people outside the church who got on boxes and stuff like that so they could see through the windows... The thought of it brings tears to my eyes even now. He was quite a guy."

### 5. They moved for me (Richard, Ch3, 00:36 to 05:34)
**Summary:** A village headteacher tells his parents their boy is bright. His father gives up the farm he loves and moves to Rugby so Richard can sit the 11 plus. Richard only worked it out after his father died. Reflective.

> "The expectation was that I would leave school at 15 and become a farm labourer... So we moved basically for me and I'm pretty humbled really when I look back at that, that my parents made that sacrifice... for my father, that was really, really significant because he loved working on the farm... They never mentioned it... It was only subsequently that I figured it out and I mentioned it to my mother and she confirmed it. But that was after my father had passed away."

### 6. "As much as you can save" (Richard, Ch4, 27:59 to 30:25)
**Summary:** Lying on the verge of the Straight Mile watching Triumph test riders at 100mph. Then asking his dad for a motorbike. The start of a love that's now 63 bikes. Daniel's favourite subject.

> "I said, "Dad, I think I'd like a motorcycle"... Much to my surprise, he said, "Yeah, I think that's a good idea"... I said, "So how much money can I have to buy a motorcycle?" And his response was, "As much as you can save." So I had the permission, but not the money... The following Saturday morning, I borrowed my father's ladders and started a window cleaning round."

### 7. Colin Chapman and the Lotus 72 (Richard, Ch6, 12:24 to 15:04)
**Summary:** Colin Chapman walks up and says "I don't know where to put the goddamn radiator." Richard digs out 1940s Spitfire papers and solves it. The car wins the championship. Daniel's McLaren friend says F1 still uses the design. History plus pride.

> "He said, "Up until now, every Formula One race car has had the radiator at the front in the nose, but it's not going to be the same on this car because that's where I want to put wings"... "I've got a problem, I don't know where to put the goddamn radiator"... I found some learned papers dated 1942, 1943, 1944 relating to cooling of the Rolls Royce Merlin engine in Spitfires and Mustangs... We perfected the cooling system on the Formula One race car. It was the Lotus 72 and its first season... it won the championship."

### 8. MI6, Iran and the blocked toilet (Richard, Ch6, 41:22 to 47:49)
**Summary:** MI6 asks Richard to look inside a guarded Iranian factory. He floods the toilets as cover, walks the floor, finds they're welding tank turrets, not bomb casings. Closes on why he said yes: his father saluting the national anthem mid-dinner. Travel, history and loyalty in one. Can split at 46:56 if too long.

> "I went into the toilet. I got hold of a load of toilet rolls, rammed them into the toilet with a broom handle... It overflowed, started running all over the floor. That was my cover story... They were repairing turrets on tanks... damaged in the Iran-Iraq war... I was back in work on Monday morning. And at 10 o'clock on Monday morning, I had a call from him. "What did you find out?""
>
> "A certain amount of excitement, a certain amount of old fashioned loyalty to the country... the thought of saying no just really didn't cross my mind... if the national anthem was played, even in the middle of his meal, my father would stand up to attention and salute."

### 9. "Love, Dad" (Richard, Ch7, 00:51 to 04:20)
**Summary:** His father never once said "I love you". Richard decided he always would. Feeding the adopted boys in the night built the bond. He gets emotional. The parenting clip, and the one Daniel raised in his own session.

> "In all the years, my father never told me that he loved me. This was the sort of stiff upper lip type approach. And I knew he did, I had no doubt that he loved me, but he never actually said it. And I determined that that would be one big difference in terms of parenting my boys... whenever I finish an email to him, it says, "Love, Dad"... not because they were adopted, I'd have done it anyway, but the fact that they were adopted made it very important... They were my sons. The bond was there... I'm getting a bit emotional for an Englishman."

### 10. The Aprilia and the goblin mask (Daniel, raw Zoom, 00:38:06 to 00:41:08)
**Summary:** Daniel never got anything new as a kid, then his dad bought him a brand new Aprilia at 17. He crashes it wearing a Halloween mask and braces for the worst. His dad's answer. Richard only heard the real reason 25 years later (he laughs about it in the Wrap Up at 39:21).

> "Dad bought me... one of the first ever Aprilia RS125s at 17 years old... it's probably one of the happiest moments of my life, this bike turning up... Until I then put a Halloween costume on my head and drove around the village. It went over my eyes, I went into the hedge, knocked myself out... I've never been so scared... But he said, "Listen, we can mend a motorbike, you know, if you really hurt yourself, you're not gonna mend." ...But I didn't tell him about the Halloween costume until about 2 years ago."

**Held back:** Ch5 44:16 to 47:24 (the 16-year-old birth mother who interviewed Richard to adopt Oliver) and Ch7 46:52 to 47:40 (Amy telling him not to sit in a black suit in the village square). Both strong but private. Check with Richard before clipping either.

### T2. Richard: "So my son would hear them" (54:24 to 56:28)
> "I really welcomed and enjoyed the possibility to sort of share my history with my son... in the midst of the hurly-burly of everyday life, you don't sit down for 6 or 7 hours and just talk about those things... In some respects, I'm a simple, thick engineer. In other respects, I'm sometimes a bit more complicated than that."
