# Snapshot Founding Five: form and call flow, v2

## The way to think about it

One structure, used three times: the form asks it, the call explores it, the prep sheet turns their answers into your running order.

Five rooms, in this order:

1. **Who it's for.** Sets the lens. Changes how you ask everything else.
2. **Your people.** The facts. Names and ages, so you never waste call time on them.
3. **How you see your life.** Their map. Which areas matter, so you know where to go deep.
4. **This year, in moments.** The heart of it. Highs, lows, the surprising bits.
5. **Right now, and next.** Where they're standing today, and what they'd want to hear back in ten years.

It starts light (ticks and names), goes deep in the middle, and ends looking forward. That's the uplift.

## The form

**Title:** Your Snapshot

**Intro:**

> Very soon, we'll sit down for an hour and a half and talk about your life as it is right now. The year you've actually had: the bits you'd tell a mate over a pint, and the bits you haven't told anyone yet.
>
> This form is how I get ready, so you arrive with more than "erm, work's been busy". There are no right answers, and a half-finished sentence is as useful to me as a polished one. Give it about 30 minutes, ideally with a cuppa and your photo roll open.
>
> Everything you write here, and everything we record, is private to you. It's yours, to keep, share or never play to anyone. That's your call.
>
> Neil

### Section 1: Who it's for

**1a. When someone presses play on your Snapshot in years to come, who do you picture listening?** (tick any)
- Me, looking back
- My children
- My partner
- My grandchildren, one day
- Other: ___

**1b. What might you want them to hear?** A story, a feeling, something you've never quite said out loud. (paragraph, optional)

### Section 2: Your people

**2. Who's in your household and close family?** Names, how they're related to you, and dates of birth if you're happy to share. (paragraph)

**3. Who else has been in your corner this year?** A friend, a colleague, a neighbour. Just names and a few words on each. (paragraph)

### Section 3: How you see your life

**4. Which of these have taken up the most room in your head this year?** (tick up to four)
- Family and kids
- Partner
- Friends
- Work
- Health, body
- Health, mind
- Faith or meaning
- Home, a move, a place
- Something you're learning or making
- Other: ___

**5. If you think about your life in a different way, chapters, seasons, a list on the fridge, tell me how.** (short answer, optional)

### Section 4: This year, in moments

**6. Scroll back through your photos from the last twelve months. Which one says "this was my year"? Tell me what's in it.** (paragraph, attach it if you like)

**7. The high point. When did you think, "this is good"?** (paragraph)

**8. The hard bit. What did you get through?** Share as much or as little as you want. Stories, not skeletons. (paragraph)

**9. What surprised you, about life or about yourself?** (paragraph)

**10. Finish the sentence: "This was the year I..."** (short answer)

### Section 5: Right now, and next

**11. What's a phrase you've said a lot this year?** The thing your family would do an impression of. (short answer)

**11b. What song captures your year? Or which artist have you been playing on repeat?** The one that goes in the background of your Snapshot. Add the song name and artist if you can. (short answer, optional)

**12. Looking twelve months ahead, what are you hoping for?** (paragraph)

**13. When you listen back in ten years, what do you hope you'll hear?** (paragraph)

**13b. Is there someone in your family you wish you'd asked more, or still could? Who are they, and what would you ask them?** (paragraph, optional. Buyer signal for the full documentary, from Steve, 7 Oct. Offer it weeks later, never quote the answer back.)

**14. Anything off limits?** (short answer, optional)

**15. One last thing, and it's entirely up to you.** I'm starting to write a book about families and the conversations we don't get round to having. If something you say could help someone else, would you be happy for me to use it, fully anonymously? No names, no places, nothing that could point back to you. (optional)
- Yes, happy for you to use it anonymously
- Ask me first
- No thanks

16 questions, 7 optional. About 25 to 30 minutes.

## The call flow, 1h30

### Before you press record (3 minutes, not recorded)

The finished audio and video is them alone. You're cut out. Brief them like this:

> "One thing that'll feel a bit odd. I'm going to disappear from the final edit, so it's just you telling your story. That means when I ask something, fold my question into your answer. If I ask 'what was the high point of your year?', don't say 'probably June'. Say 'the high point of my year was June, when...'. If you forget, I'll nudge you, no harm done."

Then do one practice: "What did you have for breakfast?" Let them get it wrong, laugh, and do it again.

### Running order

| Time | Room | What you're doing |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:05 | Warm up | Read their Q10 back to them. "Tell me about the year you..." |
| 0:05 to 0:15 | Who it's for | Who's listening, and what they want them to hear (1a, 1b). |
| 0:15 to 0:25 | Your people | Not the list. One story per key person this year. |
| 0:25 to 1:05 | This year, in moments | The photo first, then the high, the hard bit, the surprise. This is the hour of the audio. |
| 1:05 to 1:20 | Right now, and next | Their phrase, the year ahead, the ten-year listen. |
| 1:20 to 1:30 | Close | "Anything I didn't ask that I should have?" Then a message to whoever is listening, said straight to them. |

The closing message to the listener is your ending for the edit. Always get it.

## Prep sheet (one per person, filled from the form)

- **Who's listening, and what they want them to hear:** (1a, 1b)
- **Their people:** names and ages (2, 3)
- **Where to go deep:** their top areas (4)
- **The photo:** (6)
- **High / hard bit / surprise:** one line each (7 to 9)
- **Their sentence:** "This was the year I..." (10)
- **Their phrase:** (11)
- **Off limits:** (14)
- **Book consent:** (15)
- **Opening prompt:** built from Q10
- **Closing prompt:** "Say something to [listener from 1a]."

### The skill to practise: self-contained answers

The edit only works if every answer stands up without your question. Three moves, in order of how often you'll use them:

1. **Ask prompts, not questions.** "Tell me about the moment you knew the move was right" gets a full story. "Was the move the right call?" gets "yeah".
2. **Name the thing in your prompt so they repeat it.** "Talk me through the day Ella was born" hands them their first line: "The day Ella was born...".
3. **The redo.** When they answer "yeah, definitely" or start with "it was...", say: "Lovely. Give me that once more as a full sentence, as if I'm not here." Never apologise for it. After the second redo they'll do it without being asked.

When something lands, the echo still works and costs nothing in the edit: repeat their last few words back ("didn't want to go back?"), stay quiet, and cut your line. Their answer stands alone.

Tick on the sheet each time you use a redo. If you're past ten by the half-hour, your prompts need more "tell me about".

## The three openings, against the /snapshot-f5k page

The page talks about "your life as it is right now", "the story of your year" and arriving with more than "erm, work's been busy". The form should sound like the next step on that page.

- **Main (now updated):** Best. It reuses "your life as it is right now" and the "erm, work's been busy" line, so the form follows on from the page they just read. The pint line sets the tone.
- **Alt 1, "This isn't a review of your year. Nobody's marking it.":** A good line, but it pushes back against something the page never said. Use it as the second sentence if you want more warmth.
- **Alt 2, "school runs, inboxes and 'we must catch up'":** The strongest detail, but it assumes kids, and not every Founding Five has them.

Recommendation: keep the main one.
