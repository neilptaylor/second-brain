1. Tue 24 Nov: 31 days

Subject: 31 days. Father Christmas on a tin tray

Preview: For years, the children had no idea it was Dad.

Hey,

31 days to Christmas.

A dad told me about the Christmas his own father came down the stairs on a tin tray.

Christmas Day. The whole family gathered in the hall, waiting for Father Christmas, who was due at a set time.

Then a clatter.

Father Christmas, sitting on a tin tray, coming down from the half landing at speed. He usually crashed into the grandfather clock at the bottom. A white pillowcase over his shoulder, full of presents.

For years, the children had no idea it was their father.

This was a dad whose father, by his own account, had no time for religious festivals. On church Sundays he'd be in his pyjamas and dressing gown, de-heading the rhododendrons, while everyone else scrambled into their best clothes. Christmas was his wife's thing.

Except for that one hour.

I asked where the tray came from.

"I'm not sure where that came from, really."

That's what I like about it. Nobody in the family could tell me why. Only that it happened, and that it was the best part of the day.

He's the only one who can tell it, and he tells it better than I do: [FATHER CHRISTMAS ON A TRAY CLIP LINK]

Neil

2. Tue 8 Dec: 17 days

Subject: 17 days. A present for every relation

Preview: Then the tradition stopped, and his children never saw it.

Hey,

17 days to Christmas.

Same dad, another Christmas story.

Every three or four years, his mother hosted the whole wider family on Christmas Eve. Every relation, from a great-great-aunt in her nineties to the babies.

She knew all their names. She bought and wrapped a present for every single one of them, and laid them around the tree.

The children scrambled about on all fours looking for labels and delivering the presents to the right person.

Then they sang carols round the piano, with a great-aunt playing who was, in his words, "pretty dotty anyway."

It grew so big that people began to mutter.

His mother gave the second-to-last one. The other side of the family, not to be outdone, gave the last one a year later.

And that was it.

"My children have never benefited or witnessed that."

That's the line I keep thinking about. A whole tradition, ended with nobody deciding it should end. And his kids only know it exists because of a recording.

This is why I offer to weave grandchildren in. They ask what the rest of us are too polite to.

3. Tue 15 Dec: 10 days

Subject: 10 days. A Hawker Hunter with no cockpit

Preview: He could still name the plane, seventy years on.

Hey,

10 days to Christmas.

I asked a man in his seventies what he wanted for Christmas when he was a boy.

A football, he said.

He remembered being at the market, being asked what he'd like, and his mum steering him away so his dad could go and "send it off to Father Christmas."

Then, without me asking, another Christmas. An Airfix kit of a Hawker Hunter jet.

He could still name the plane, seventy years on.

He'd built most of it. He left out the little bits inside the cockpit, because he couldn't be bothered. "I think my mum and dad were quite upset about that."

One question. A toy, a market, a dad, and a mum who knew what she was doing.

You'll be sitting with your parent at some point in the next fortnight. Try this one, then stop talking: "What did you want for Christmas when you were eight, and did you get it?"
