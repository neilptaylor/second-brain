The Front Row: Hormozi review and the fix

8 October 2026. Scored against what Hormozi has taught on his YouTube channel from January to October 2026, using Steve's corpus. Every point below is Hormozi speaking, not a guest. These fixes override the pricing in the Front Row offer doc v2.

Score: 5 out of 10

What he'd like

  • The annual session is the real prize. He tells a service business to list a membership at $500 and drop it to $200 in exchange for a review. The money isn't the point. The point is a guaranteed yearly appointment where you're in front of the customer to sell more ("How I Would Build a $10M Service Business", 29 Aug 2026, 13:10). The Front Row puts you in front of ten people once a year for five years. That's the best thing about it.
  • There's a reason why. He says every discount needs a stated reason, or it reads as suspicious ("Can I Save This Failing Theme Park in 90 Days?", 11 Sep 2026, 12:28). "You're the first in, you help me build it, and you share it" does that job.

Where it loses marks

  • It's a discount, not a bigger offer. His advice to a practice whose problem was getting customers: ask for referrals at every visit, and give away something big rather than cutting the price ("8 Entrepreneurs Compete For $100,000", 2 Oct 2026, 2:51). At £99 with video in, each Snapshot costs you £170 of your time. You're paying £71 a year, per person, for the privilege.
  • Paid yearly means five chances to walk away. His rule 11: always offer at least 10% off for paying in full today, because a prepaid customer can't churn and the cash arrives now ("The Mathematics of Business, Explained", 9 Jan 2026, 47:59). He also says every business of his that got big got its acquisition cost back inside 30 days (same video, 53:47). At £99 a year you're waiting five years to get it back.
  • The friend link is a second discount on top of the first. At £249 a referred friend leaves you £54, so every Front Row member needs about 6.6 bookings to cover what they cost you. Nobody brings six people.
  • Selling out of your own wallet. He says owners undercharge because what they do comes easily to them, and that the fix is to price off what the result is worth to the customer ("The Mathematics of Business, Explained", 9 Jan 2026, 34:54). An hour of your dad's voice on record isn't worth £99.

The fix

  • Sell it as £495 for five years, paid today. The headline doesn't change: it's still £99 a year. But the money lands now and nobody drops out in year three. That's £4,950 in the bank in October, not £990.
  • Paying yearly is the fallback, at £149 a year. Only offer it to someone who says no to paying up front. He says to keep the payment plan as the downsell and never lead with it (same video, 48:59).
  • Friends pay the full £299, and get the video free. Video is normally £50 on top. That's giving something big rather than cutting the price, and it leaves you £79 a booking after the £50 referral, not £54. The gap per Front Row member drops from 6.6 bookings to about 4.5.
  • Build the family documentary into the yearly session. Use the last ten minutes of every Front Row session to ask: "Is there someone in your family whose story you'd want on record?" One Hero Story at £2,400 LIST, at the bottom of the 60% to 66% margin band, leaves about £1,440. That one sale covers four Front Row members' gaps. Three of them cover the whole cohort.
  • Keep everything else. Ten places, the application form, Steve's question, the deadline, and £50 for every booking through their link.

With the fix: 8 out of 10. It still loses money on the Snapshots on their own. It works because the cash comes in on day one, the referral pays for itself faster, and the yearly session turns into the door to a family documentary. That last part is the business case. Without it, the Front Row is just marketing spend.
