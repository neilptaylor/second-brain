My Story

The conversation I never got to have.

Nobody stopped loving each other. Life just got in the way.

The conversation I never got to have

My dad, Patrick, passed away on a Monday. My son was born the following Monday. His middle name is Patrick. They'll never share the same room.

I can tell my boy that every Sunday morning, Dad religiously polished my football boots. That he made pancakes in animal shapes. That he told me to chew them properly — and I still have no idea why. I can tell him that Patrick was born in Thailand, that he took a boat to boarding school at eight with only his five-year-old brother for company, that he lost the sight in one eye at eleven.

I know all of that. What I don't know is how any of it felt. Or how it shaped the man who shaped me.

Those stories, in his voice, are gone. For me. For my son. For his kids one day.

That absence is what pushed me to build this. Not a recording service. Not a memoir project. A guided experience that gets families to sit down — properly sit down — and make time for the stories that matter, while the people who lived them are still in the room.

Why I built Me & My Old Man

I'm British, based near Barcelona, and I've spent the last few years sitting with families across 7 countries — 38 of them so far, 400+ hours of recorded life.

What I've learned is that the audiobook, in the end, is almost beside the point. Families come to me because they want a keepsake in their parent's voice. They leave with something they weren't expecting: a different relationship. Siblings who talked properly for the first time in years. A dad who felt heard maybe for the first time. A quiet permission to be a family again.

How I work with families

The work is high-touch and gently paced. I take a small number of families at a time. There's no checkout, no packages page, no hard sell. Everything starts with a short, free call — you tell me about your family, I tell you honestly whether this is something I can help with.

If any of this sounds like your family, I'd love to hear from you.

You always assume there's more time. There isn't. Give me twenty minutes and I'll tell you what's possible.

The questions families ask me

Most people who find this page have been meaning to ask their mum or dad something for years.

These are the questions I hear on discovery calls, nearly word for word. If yours isn't here, ask me on the call. It's 20 minutes, and it's free.

What this actually is

What actually happens? Is it a book, a podcast, a recording?

It's an audio documentary of your parent's life, in their own voice.

I sit down with your mum or dad for three two-hour conversations. Not a quiz, not a form. More like a long chat with someone who's very good at listening and asking "and then what happened?" Depending on the shape you pick, I also spend an hour with each of their children first, so your memories become my questions.

Then it all gets edited into chapters, with music, the way you'd hear a radio documentary. Your childhood memories, that special holiday, and that story you think they'll have forgotten. Your dad talking about the first car he took apart on the drive. Your mum explaining why she really moved the whole family to Australia.

Is it private? Will other people be able to listen to it?

Yes, it's private. Completely.

People often hear "audio" and picture a podcast that strangers will stream. It isn't that. Your family's documentary goes to your family, and nobody else. It isn't published anywhere, it isn't on Spotify, and I never share a word of it without asking you first.

That's what lets people open up. Your dad isn't performing for an audience. He's talking to his children, with me in the room to keep the conversation going.

If you ever want to share a clip with your wider family, that's your call, not mine.

Why audio, and not video or a book?

Because a voice does something a page can't.

Ask anyone who's lost a parent what they'd give to hear them again. Not read them. Hear them. The laugh halfway through a sentence. The way they said your name. As one client put it: "I can't wait to listen to these every year for the next 50 years. To hear his voice."

And what I miss most, after a hug, would be to hear my dad's voice. His stories.

Audio is also kinder in the room. There's no camera pointing at anyone, no worrying about hair or lighting. People forget they're being recorded within about ten minutes. That's when the good stuff comes out.

Who it's for

Who is this for?

Usually it's someone in their forties or fifties who's noticed their phone calls with their mum or dad have shrunk to the weather and the boiler. They love them completely. They just realise they don't know their whole story.

Some come to me because a parent is turning 70 or 80 and they want to do something better than another jumper. Some come because there's been a health scare. Some have already lost one parent and won't let it happen twice.

It works for one parent or both, one child or five, close families and families with a bit of distance in them.

Do you only do parents? I have something a bit different in mind.

No. Some of my most important work hasn't been a parent at all.

I've captured the life story of a husband and father, after he'd passed away, for the children who'll never meet him. I interviewed his wife, his two best mates, his parents and his brothers, and together they built a picture of the whole man.

For my sister's 50th, I created an audio documentary of her life through the stories of her childhood friends, her school pals, and the people who've played a special role in her life. She found it hard to describe how much it meant to her.

If what you have in mind doesn't fit a neat box, tell me about it on a call. Those projects tend to be the ones that matter most.

Tell me what you have in mind

My parent has already passed away. Is it too late?

No. It's different, but it isn't too late.

When someone's gone, their story doesn't disappear. It gets scattered. A bit with their sister, a bit with an old friend, a bit with the neighbour who knew them before you were born. I gather those people together and help them tell it.
