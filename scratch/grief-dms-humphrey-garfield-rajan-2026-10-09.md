## Andrew Garfield, Instagram DM

Andrew, I've watched you talk about your mum more times than I'd admit to.

Most people go quiet around grief, or reach for something tidy. You don't. You find the words for it, out loud, and somehow make the room feel safer for it. That's a rare kind of emotional intelligence, and it gives a lot of people permission.

I make audio documentaries of people's lives. Lately, more and more of them are for someone who's already gone.

The first was for a man who died before he could meet his children. His wife fought through the courts to have them anyway. So I interviewed the people who knew him best: his parents, his brothers, his closest mates, and her. His laugh. His daft guitar songs. Now when the kids ask "what was Dad like?", there's an answer, in the voices of the people who loved him.

When she heard it back, she said, "He was real."

It made me think of you and your mum. If you ever wanted to hear her through the people who knew her, I'd be honoured to make it.

Neil

**Subject:** Your Desert Island Discs with your mum

Amol,

You did Desert Island Discs with your mum, and told everyone else to do the same with theirs.

I'd guess plenty of your readers meant to. Then it was a Tuesday night, Mum was on the phone about the boiler, and where do you even start?

That's the bit I do. I interview a parent over a few weeks, then their children, and edit it into an audio documentary of their life, in their own voice. 37 families so far. The thing they say most isn't about the recording, it's "it brought us closer." One client said nobody had ever given their dad that much attention without interrupting him.

My dad died in 2021, six days before my son was born. I never recorded him. That's why it exists.

If it's ever useful to you or ARC, I'd love to tell you more.

Neil

P.S. I also made one for a man who'd already died, so his two children, born after he'd gone, would know who he was. National Grief Awareness Week starts 2 December, if that's ever a story for you.
