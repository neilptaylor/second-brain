**Lecture Title**
The Dishwasher Is the Problem: Why We Never Ask Our Parents the Big Questions

**Lecture Description**
I once rang my mum and the whole call, all twelve minutes of it, was about the dishwasher. That's the level most of us are at with the people we love most. We talk logistics, the boiler, the weather, and then someone says "right, I'd better go".

This is a short, warm talk about why. Nobody stopped loving anyone. Families drift, slowly and without anyone noticing, and by the time you want to ask the proper questions, the gateway to asking them has quietly closed. I'll talk about what sits underneath the small talk, why a parent can be a brilliant storyteller at a dinner party and a closed book at home, and what happens when siblings listen to each other tell the same story about the same parent for the first time in years.

It comes from 390+ hours of recorded interviews across 38 families in 11 countries. Expect a few laughs (the dishwasher gets a hard time), one or two moments where the room goes quiet, and a practical question or two you can take home and ask this week. No slides needed, just a pint and a bit of honesty.

**Speaker Bio**
Neil Taylor is the founder of Me & My Old Man. When his dad died in 2021, he realised how much he'd never asked, and that his voice was already fading from memory. His son was born six days later, and the two never met. In 2024 he started recording families, sitting down with a parent, and with their children, to capture a life story in their own voice as an audio documentary. Since then he has done 390+ hours of interviews across 38 families in 11 countries. He writes and speaks about the sandwich generation, the slow drift between parents and grown-up children, and the ordinary conversations that quietly decide whether we ever have the extraordinary ones. He lives near Barcelona.
