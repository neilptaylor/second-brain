# $100M Money Models: working digest

Source: Alex Hormozi, *$100M Money Models* (2025). Full PDF in Drive `02 Hormozi Books/`, file ID `1-ReZDnL2p0NQ21Es42nK-prwg7neDA7s`. Read in full on 12 Oct 2026. This is a digest in our words, not the book. For exact scripts or numbers, extract the PDF with `pdftotext` and quote from there. Section and chapter names below match the book's contents page.

## The core idea

A Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers: what you offer, when, and how. The test is cash, not revenue.

- Good: profit from one customer in the first 30 days covers the cost of getting and serving that customer.
- $100M: profit from one customer in 30 days covers getting and serving at least two more. Cash stops limiting growth.
- Why 30 days: a business card gives roughly 30 days of interest-free float, so the same money can be used again.
- Failure pattern: slow-drip profit, so a business that cannot afford customers cuts ads, starves itself, and borrows.
- Build in stages, one offer at a time, each stage paying for the next:
  1. Get Cash: attraction offers.
  2. Get More Cash: upsells and downsells.
  3. Get The Most Cash: continuity.
  Do not try to install the whole model at once. It breaks the business. Measure in quarters.

## The four offer types

| Type | Job |
|---|---|
| Attraction | Turn strangers into customers, ideally cash-positive |
| Upsell | Whatever you offer next: more, better, or different |
| Downsell | Whatever you offer after a "no". Never the same thing for less |
| Continuity | Ongoing value for ongoing payment |

## Attraction offers (Section II)

Free, discount and $1 sit on one continuum. Free gets more leads. A discount gets fewer leads but a higher show-up rate (use it where no-shows are costly).

1. **Win Your Money Back.** Deposit now, get it back (cash or credit) for hitting results, taking actions, or both. Criteria must be easy to track, likely to produce the result, and advertise the business (reviews, posts, referrals). Make check-in calls part of the criteria because each one is an upsell moment. Apply won credit over a longer term (for example £600 as £50 off per month for 12 months) so they keep paying something. Make everyone a winner in private: credit the deposit forward even if they miss. About 10% ask for refunds. Only use it if refunds are below 5%. The money is made on the people who succeed and have something else to buy.
2. **Giveaways.** Advertise a big prize with a stated value. Collect contact details and qualifying answers. Everyone else gets a "partial scholarship" or voucher on the core offer, ideally 10 to 30% of gross margin. Deadlines at three points: enter, claim, use. Grand prize must be genuinely grand or nobody enters. Someone must actually win. Referral twist: refer a winner and win too. Heavily regulated, check the law.
3. **Decoy Offer.** Advertise a stripped-down free or cheap version. When they enquire, show the premium next to it and make the contrast huge. Ask "are you here for free stuff or lasting results?" to get permission to lead with premium. Advertise benefits, not features. A surprise extra for decoy takers builds goodwill for later upsells.
4. **Buy X Get Y Free.** Free beats discount. Triple the price and add two more items, or "buy 6 months, get 12 free" at the same total. Raise prices for real first. More free than paid works better. Can be sold to existing customers (cap at about 10%) for a cash pop. Budget the cash so you can deliver what you prepaid.
5. **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later.** Card on file, pay nothing now and be billed the full price later unless a clear yes/no promise is not met. Or pay a discounted price (20 to 30% or more, plus bonuses) now. Conditional guarantee, for example you must attend to cancel. If more than 10% cancel, you over-promised or priced too high. Hint at your next offer early in any workshop.
6. **Free Goodwill Offer.** The book's own review ask. Not a mechanism to copy.

## Upsells (Section III)

Upsells fail when the offer is unwanted, mistimed, or not believed. The first offer is often not where the profit is (burger, then fries, then drink, then supersize).

1. **Classic Upsell.** "You can't have X without Y." Offer the fix for the next problem the moment it appears. Ask "you don't want anything else, do you?" so a "no" means yes. Highest-profit option first. Add bonuses one at a time, then give all of them anyway. Faster access raises perceived value. Name bundles. Book the next meeting from every meeting. Buyers in a "hyper buying" window (weddings, babies, moving) spend most. Price the middle option to steer people up. Charge for guarantees and insurance (5 to 50%).
2. **Menu Upsell.** Four moves: unsell what they do not need, prescribe what they do (personalised, step by step), ask A or B rather than yes or no, then "use the card on file?" Add a nudge ("this is my favourite"). Staff can unsell openly. If you are out of stock, take payment and set a delivery date. When you want both options bought, add a third "both" option priced the same as the dearer one.
3. **Anchor Upsell.** Premium first at 5 to 10 times the price. Wait for the gasp. Ask what they care about, then present the main offer with the same primary features, secondary ones changed. Only works if the premium is a real offer you would deliver. Having it ready also stops you under-selling someone with a bigger budget.
4. **Rollover Upsell.** Credit some or all of a previous purchase toward a larger next one. Use on lapsed customers, unhappy customers (before a refund), other firms' unhappy customers, and current ones. Price the next offer at least 4x the credit (25% max discount). One-time, now-only for urgency. Winback example in the book: personalised videos to 200 past customers, about 20% took it. Also: a gift-card play where a $200 card sells for $20, rolled into a $1,000+ offer.

## Downsells (Section IV)

Rules: they said no to this offer, not to you. Trade, do not cave. Personalise, do not pressure. Never drop the price just to close, because customers talk and trust goes. "Same thing for less" is discounting. Different thing for less, or same thing paid differently, is a downsell.

1. **Payment Plan Downsell.** Same price, different timing. Order of steps: reward paying in full (price includes the interest, discount for prepay), third-party finance or card or layaway, half now and half on payday, check they still want it (1 to 10, need 8+), thirds, evenly spread, then a trial. Schedule on paydays. Keep offering the prepay discount mid-plan. Success test: closes go up while the share paying in full stays the same. Annual billing churns about 2% a month against about 10.7% monthly (ProfitWell figures in the book).
2. **Trial With Penalty.** Free if they do the things that make a great customer (same criteria as Win Your Money Back), otherwise they pay. Card on file first, explain fees after. Required check-ins double as upsell calls. Handle liked, hated and unused trials differently. Call it "a free trial". Used as a downsell for recurring services where the customer has to do the work.
3. **Feature Downsell.** Remove a feature, drop the price, ask "how about now?" Remove highest-value features first so people re-upsell themselves (the guarantee is the famous one). Levers: quantity, quality, speed, access, DIY versus done-with-you versus done-for-you, personalisation. Name the tiers (aspirational top, "The Minimum" bottom). Ask "deal?" or "fair enough?" after each. Barter discounts for reviews, testimonials and introductions. Downsell current customers who are not using a feature before they cancel.

## Continuity (Section V)

Use it last, not as the lead offer, because it crushes 30-day cash. Always something to upsell around it.

1. **Continuity Bonus.** Sell the bonus first, anchor its real value, then reveal it is free with membership. Bonuses related to the core offer and things you already have. To push people into continuity, price the one-off option 1.33x to 2.66x higher (50% to 90% choose continuity). Bulk prepay discounts lift 30-day cash. Titles (silver, gold) work as a bonus.
2. **Continuity Discount.** Free time for committing. Apply up front, at the end, spread, or after the first payments. Extend the term rather than shortening it. Bill every four weeks, not monthly (13 cycles a year, +8.3%). Add a processing fee and offer to waive it for a second payment method. Lifetime discount unlocked at your usual churn month. Make the cancellation fee equal the discount received. Make cancelling easy and ask for an exit interview (fee waived in exchange).
3. **Waived Fee.** Month-to-month with a large set-up fee (3 to 5x monthly), waived for a 12-month commitment, payable if they leave early. Smaller fee for more up-front cash. If more than 5% want out early, look at the product.

## Making the model (Section VI)

- Order: Attraction, then Upsell, then Downsell, then Continuity. Perfect one offer until it is reliable before the next.
- Fewer products, more ways to offer them. Affiliate products can fill gaps.
- Price low for early yeses and feedback, then raise until revenue falls.
- Turn bulk attraction offers into continuity with automatic renewal.
- Worked examples in the book: Gym Launch (decoy, classic upsell, payment plan, menu plus feature downsell), a gym challenge (win your money back, thirds, menu upsell, rollover), a newsletter, dog food.
