### FEAR 1: THE TICKING CLOCK
*"I'm running out of time and I know it"*

The most common entry point. A health scare, a birthday milestone, or watching a parent's memory slip creates sudden urgency. The buyer has been meaning to do this for years. Something external forces the moment.

**VERBATIM — EXISTING (Feb 2026 baseline):**
- "Before it's too late." — Multiple sources (highest frequency phrase in the corpus)
- "I'm 78 now. So I thought I'd better do something before I go gaga." — **Colin Best** *(NEW — June 2026)*
- "She is deteriorating." — Georgina Warren *(delivery note)*
- "He's had a huge amount of support... he must be coming up to nine lives." — **Claire Bunce** *(NEW — June 2026, on her dad)*

**VERBATIM — NEW (June 2026):**
- "Both his parents died of Alzheimer's. So he'll be super aware of like how precious memory is and stories." — **Claire Bunce** (on her father's awareness of his own risk)
- "The time between recording and then coming... if she was in a more advanced period of decline, like there's... I imagine you triage people on that basis." — **Georgina Warren** (on urgency)
- "Dad hasn't been very well recently. And I was thinking about this originally for him because I think his memory's slightly going." — **Claire Bunce**
- "He's had a huge amount of health scares... keeps kind of bouncing back." — **Claire Bunce**

**NEW SUB-CATEGORY — THE HEALTH SCARE WINDOW:**
Vaughan Griffiths (Geoff's father) had a heart bypass. It softened him. Created a window of emotional access that wasn't there before. This is a distinct buying trigger: not "before they die" but "while they're temporarily open."
- "He had a massive heart operation, a bypass. And since then, he's just been a bit more... he's been a bit softer in many ways." — **Geoff Griffiths**
- "He's never actually talked to anybody much about his feelings before this." — **Geoff Griffiths**

### FEAR 2: THE DIY TRAP
*"I keep meaning to do this myself. I know I never will."*

The buyer has already tried. The written biography, the video camera, the Desert Island Discs at the 70th birthday. All fell flat. Either dad deflected, or it took years and went nowhere, or the format was wrong.

**VERBATIM — EXISTING:**
- "I tried to do a biography with him written down... it was back and forth really slow. He just wouldn't really answer the question and tell the story." — **Neil** (selling story)
- "Eight years later he died and I'd only got 10 pages down." — **Neil**

**VERBATIM — NEW (June 2026):**
- "It's fairly flat. It's flat because writing is quite flat, usually." — **Colin Best** (on his own written memoirs — THE most powerful DIY trap quote in the corpus; he has done the work and still knows it isn't enough)
- "We did a little thing for his 70th. I got everyone to record a Desert Island Discs. And he just, he talked about the music. He didn't talk about himself." — **Geoff Griffiths** (prior DIY attempt that failed — dad deflected to content rather than self-disclosure)
- "I hadn't thought about, and I also hadn't thought about, because I'd not really heard that this was a thing." — **Claire Bunce** (awareness gap — not laziness, simply didn't know this service category existed)

**NEW PATTERN — THE DEFLECTING DAD:**
Some dads are perfectly willing to participate in a recording exercise — they just talk about everything except themselves. They hold forth on other people, on events, on music. The children leave knowing less about the dad than they hoped.

### FEAR 3: THE CONVERSATION BARRIER
*"I can't get past surface level with them"*

Distance, busyness, domestic noise. But also — and this is the new data — emotional closure in the family system. Some families simply don't do feelings. The buyer is often the only one who wants to go deeper.

**VERBATIM — EXISTING:**
- "We talk about the dishwasher. That's the level we're at." — **Brittany**
- "My dad's a closed book, right? He's a massive storyteller, but all his stories are comedic snippets of other people." — **Andy Fahey**

**VERBATIM — NEW (June 2026):**
- "We were never in like an open family. I feel like I'm the only person in my family that like shares his feelings." — **Chuck Baumberg** (emotionally closed family system — buyer is the outlier, not the norm)
- "None of them talk properly with each other." — **Geoff Griffiths**
- "I've literally said to him, like, dad, I don't know anything about your, how you grew up. And like, I don't really know anything." — **Geoff Griffiths** (the direct attempt that goes nowhere)
- "He would only ever talk about his dad within the lens of a thing that he's comfortable talking about." — **Geoff Griffiths** (deflection pattern: talking through a proxy topic)
- "He's never said anything to anybody about his dad. There's clearly trauma." — **Geoff Griffiths**
- "She just felt that it was for wider audiences, rather than something kind of pretty emotional and really for... selfishly for me." — **Georgina Warren** (mum misread the format as a public interview, not a private family document — process issue worth noting)

### FEAR 4: THE REGRET
*"I've already lost one person. I know what it feels like not to have this."*

The grief-converted buyer. Often triggered by a bereavement. Less common but highest emotional intensity and fastest to convert.

**VERBATIM — EXISTING:**

**VERBATIM — NEW (June 2026):**

*"We've drifted. Life just got in the way."*

The sandwich generation default. So busy managing two directions (kids + parents) that quality connection with the generation above has quietly died. Not estrangement — just drift.

**VERBATIM — EXISTING:**
- "Nobody stopped loving each other. Life just got in the way." — **Neil** (signature phrase)

**VERBATIM — NEW (June 2026):**
- "We don't really hang out anymore." — **Chuck Baumberg** (about his brother — applies equally to parent-child)
- "Maybe we have dinner together once or twice a year." — **Chuck Baumberg**
- "They've got busy lives. They're both working hard. So I thought, eventually, I'm going to have to take the initiative here." — **Colin Best** (parent's perspective: watching children drift, deciding to act himself)
- "Sometimes we don't want to hear the stories, but it's nice knowing that they are there when we do." — **Abigael Smith** (the paradox of family life: we know the stories are there, we just don't stop to receive them)

## PART 5: CONTENT RESONANCE RULES
*What works, what doesn't, what to always and never do.*

**ALWAYS:**
- Lead with a specific, domestic, real detail (the dishwasher, the fluffy tufts, the voice on the train)
- Use "we" — you're in this together, not pointing fingers
- Let the reader arrive at the emotional conclusion themselves — don't name it for them
- Show the gap between what we have (photos, flat memoirs) and what's possible (voice, full story)
- Reference the specific moment something was missed — the funeral eulogy, the empty chair, the photo without a story behind it

**NEVER:**
- Use: preserve, legacy (mostly), heirloom, keepsake, treasure, for posterity, process, "stopped me cold," "sit with," "hold space for"
- Start with abstract emotional claims ("life is short," "families drift apart")
- Blame the reader for the drift
- End in darkness — pain is the doorway, never the destination

**NEW RULES — June 2026 data:**

- **The Funeral Revelation is a content angle.** Stuart Greaney named it perfectly: "It's a bizarre human approach that you don't talk about until they're dead. And then they're like, oh, and here's all the stuff they did." Use this. Post it. It's universally recognisable.
- **The Written Memoir Problem is a pre-emptive objection handler.** Colin Best's line — "It's fairly flat. Writing is quite flat, usually." — validates the audio format superiority without dismissing the effort someone has already made.
- **The Voice is sacred.** Colin Best didn't have Doreen's voice. That's the loss that lives. Every piece of content should implicitly answer: what would you give to hear them again?
- **The Parent-as-Person Awakening is a specific buyer trigger.** Chuck and Geoff both hit it: becoming a parent themselves, or watching a parent become vulnerable, makes them suddenly curious about who their parent was *as a person*. Not as a parent. This is a distinct, underserved emotional entry point.
- **Becoming a parent is a trigger.** "Having a kid... now I'm just curious to know... her thought process in having my brother and I." — Chuck Baumberg. This is a content series: *the year you became a parent and started wondering about theirs.*
- **The Sandwich Generation language is mainstream now.** 63 million US adults, ~16 million in the sandwich generation (AARP 2025). The language is in mainstream media (Aging Untold, April 2026). Neil can use this framing without it feeling niche.

## NEW SECTION A: FEAR 8 — THE DEFLECTION PATTERN
*"I've tried to ask. He answers something else entirely."*

Emerging strongly from the June 2026 sales calls. Distinct from Fear 3 (Conversation Barrier). This isn't emotional closure — it's a specific deflection mechanism where the parent redirects every personal question through a proxy topic (music, genealogy, facts). The child feels heard but learns nothing. It normalises over years. Children stop asking because they've learned the pattern without naming it.

**VERBATIM — NEW (June 2026):**
- "He would only ever talk about his dad within the lens of a thing that he's comfortable talking about. So he'd be like, oh, well, my family were very musical." — **Geoff Griffiths**, June 12, 2026
- "He just wouldn't really answer the question and tell the story." — **Neil** (selling story — ref. written biography attempt)
- "The actual point of Desert Island Discs was kind of just ignored. And then we were like, well, I guess it was interesting, but I don't give a sh*t about Shostakovich." — **Geoff Griffiths**, June 12, 2026
- "He talked about all these people, about how they, what instruments they played. I don't know anything about these people." — **Geoff Griffiths**, June 12, 2026
- "I don't think anyone said, because the thing is when I said to him about, when he did all the family tree stuff… none of them actually worked. Because they're not actually being done with alignment on why they're being done." — **Geoff Griffiths**, June 12, 2026

*INSIGHT: This is a powerful content angle and a pre-emptive objection handler. Families who've tried Desert Island Discs, the written memoir, the birthday video — they've done the DIY version and it gave them something, just not what they actually wanted. The deflection to content (music, facts, genealogy) is the dad's defence mechanism. An objective third party with a specific brief breaks it precisely because there's no relationship dynamic to protect.*
