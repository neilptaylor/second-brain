# Chapter title style guide + approved examples

Source: Neil's "Audiobook Chapter Title Creator" Claude project, Files section —
"Chapter Titles Approved and Sent to Client for reference" (last updated 24 August 2026).
This is the full instruction set that project runs on; the editor-briefing skill's
Step 3 (Propose chapter titles) follows the same rules for parent sessions.

## Instructions (verbatim)

Act as my podcast producer. Take interview transcripts and suggest Chapter Titles
using my unique style.

Guidance:
- Note the length, style, level of specifics, desire to make it intriguing.
- Note the effort for alliteration but not overdoing it (1-2 examples)
- Note the rhythm of short word + short word + ampersand for the last part of the title.
- They are in chronological order of when mentioned in the interview
- Provide 3 examples for each transcript shared
- Provide 1-line reasoning for the inclusion. Tell me succinctly the story behind each one
- Look to include verbatim words or phrases but only 1 across the whole set of chapters.
  2 max per Chapter Title. These are nice because it brings in the interviewee's
  personality, the way they say things, and will remind the listener (their family)
  of them.
- Take your time.

Additional rule folded in from Neil's briefing (2026-08-24): cover the full extent
of the chapter — ideally a 1-2 word/phrase for beginning, middle, and end — but the
weight of the story should decide which beats actually make the cut, not mechanical
even spacing. 10 words max per title.

## Wrap Up chapters

The Cheyneys and Lesley Marsh sets above both close with a dedicated Wrap Up
chapter, titled simply "Wrap Up & Reflections" (or "Family Wrap Up &
Reflections") rather than a beat-list title — this is a fixed label, not a
generated one, for whichever chapter covers the family's closing/reflections
session (always the final chapter, after every child and the parent have been
recorded). Step 3 of the editor-briefing skill applies this fixed formula
directly, the same way it does for a Child Briefing — no beat-list options
generated for it.

## Fuller worked example, with reasoning (Guy Cheyney S1, Chapter 1 — three options)

Covers: birth in Cape Town, lost brothers, father's family rift (the banned
wedding), Schumann's Arabesque, Malaya war grave trip, mother the mannequin,
maternal grandfather, only-child mischief, the bomb, the clothes brush.

**Option A:** Ch[X]. Cape Town Afterthought, The Banned Wedding, Schumann's Arabesque,
A Grave in Malaya, The Mannequin, Drooling Labradors & The Clothes Brush
Reasoning: "Afterthought" is Guy's own word — "I was very much an afterthought."
"Drooling Labrador" is verbatim — his grandfather drinking rum and raw eggs. Both
capture his dry, self-deprecating voice. Strong chronological arc from birth through
parents to childhood discipline.

**Option B:** Ch[X]. 'Lucky, Not a Miracle', The 1939 Rift, Schumann's Arabesque, One
Ship Out of Eleven, The Mannequin, Bats, Bombs & The Clothes Brush
Reasoning: Opens with Guy's verbatim correction to Neil — "I hardly describe it as
that... Lucky. Lucky is the way I would put it." "One Ship Out of Eleven" is the
father's survival story. "Bats, Bombs" gives alliteration and captures the mischief.
Ends with the signature object.

**Option C:** Ch[X]. Cape Town Caesarean, Bonkers Grandfathers, The Wrong Church,
Schumann's Arabesque, Malaya, The Mannequin & Raw Eggs with Grandfather
Reasoning: "Bonkers" is near-verbatim — "my grandfather, who was probably slightly
bonkers." "The Wrong Church" is the darkly comic funeral story. "Raw Eggs with
Grandfather" is the warm counterpoint. Two grandfather stories bookend the family
dynamics.

Note how each option threads the same underlying material differently — same
chapter, different chosen beats and different verbatim anchor — which is why Step 3
generates 3-4 distinct options per chapter rather than one "best" title.
