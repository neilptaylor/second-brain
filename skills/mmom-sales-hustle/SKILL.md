# MMOM Sales Hustle

Neil's problem is never wording. It is deciding how hard to push, and worrying what people think. This skill takes that decision out of his head. The count decides, not his mood.

## Read first, every run

1. `knowledge/offer/follow-up-playbook.md`. The ladder, the plays, the call script and the running rules live there. Do not work from memory of it.
2. `knowledge/voice/VOICE_PROFILE_Neil_Taylor.md` in full, and `knowledge/lessons.md`. Load `mmom-voice-check` before drafting a word.
3. `knowledge/offer/pricing.md` before any figure is said. A skill never holds a price.

## The method

**1. Run the roster scan (see "The daily run" below).** Never build the list from Follow-up Date alone: about 40 live leads have no date and a due-date filter silently drops them. Airtable is a "who to check" list, never "what to send". Read the real latest thread before drafting, and gather it yourself (Gmail, Calendar, Fathom, Drive, scratch/) before asking Neil for anything.

**2. Read the temperature, in one line, before any draft.** Moving or stalled. Who they are to Neil (friend or lead). What they last said, and in what tone. The real next step. Show Neil the line. A lead who has moved in the last 7 days (booked or held a call, a parent or partner engaged) is moving: warm nudge on the next step, never a go/no-go. The decision belongs after the call that answers their questions.

**3. Pick the rung.** Three touches, then the fork (real date, true reason), then one exit message, then Lapsed. A lead older than 6 weeks that is standing still skips to the fork. Count touches from the thread, not from feel.

**4. Pick the shortcut that beats chasing.**
- Send the invite, not the question. Silence means no, a moved invite means yes.
- A diary link for the elusive, ending on the link. Never "I'll park it".
- Send the invite now to someone who is away.
- Exact price goes on a call. If they ask "how much" in a message, answer with the per-parent range from `pricing.md` (Hero Story figure up to the top Full Chorus figure for one parent), then the call for their figure. Own a deck that went out first only if the lead raised it or it is why they went quiet, never as a reflex.

**5. Draft** to the rung brief below, in the voice rules and output format of `mmom-follow-up` (banned phrases, friend or lead table, subject lines written like chapter titles, two alternative openers). One question per message, answerable with a word or a date. Never "checking in", never flag-and-deny ("no pressure", "not chasing"), never a parent's illness as a reason to pause, no lead magnet for a hot lead. Echo what the lead actually said, in their words, and never credit them with an action they have not taken ("as I thought about how to sell it to my folks" is thinking, not "how you've been trying to sell it"). Mark Douglas, 8 Oct 2026.

## What each rung's message carries

- **Touch 1:** the specific next step and two real times. One detail from their call so it could only be to them ("been thinking about what you said about your mum's laugh"). No re-pitch, no price.
- **Touch 2:** one new thing, never the same message again: a detail from their call, a real family story, an answer to a worry they raised. Plus the same step.
- **Touch 3, the fork:** "Do you want to take this forward, or park it?" with a real date and the true reason. Diary-link version ends on the link.
- **Exit message:** one line, warm, leaves the door open. Then Lapsed.
- **Re-opening a Lapsed lead Neil wants to try again:** read the temperature fresh. If the old block was timing or another person, name it from their own words and ask whether it has moved. Never write an own-the-deck line ("a deck's a rubbish way to see the options, I should have talked you through it") unless they raised the deck themselves: it casts them as waiting on Neil when they are getting on with their life. For a friend, open warm with a real line about the season or their week, circle back, name the block from their own words, say there are now a few ways in that suit a busy life, then one call ask with loose windows ("Mon and Wed AM next week, let me know"). Brittany Gregory, 15 Oct 2026.
- **Lapsed and staying lapsed:** the no-ask drip, written with `mmom-follow-up` Mode 3. Never more than one message every 4 to 6 weeks.
- **Lead parks it with an apology:** short shrug back, confirm the date and his own move, no replaying their situation. If the block is busy siblings or timing, a P.S. naming Hero Story as the lighter shape (undersold, with his own reason). If it is money, leave the lighter option out. Full wording in `mmom-follow-up` voice rules (Alison, 7 Oct 2026).
- A WhatsApp voice note instead of text: `mmom-follow-up` Mode 2.

Never two asks in a row. Never "did you get my proposal?". Never apologise for following up. Never discount to break a silence.

**6. If it's a call**, use the call script in the playbook.

**7. Close the loop, same turn.**
- Airtable: Next Line, Follow-up Date, Last Action and its date once Neil says it has gone. Lapsed is the Temp field.
- Google Calendar: every parked lead gets a 9am reminder on its date with the drafted message in the description.
- Save drafts to `scratch/` as `.md` if more than two.
- Log any correction in `knowledge/lessons.md` the same turn and edit the right section here or in the playbook, bump the version comment.

## The daily run (added 15 Oct 2026)

Neil never names a lead, pastes a thread or asks "who else?". Triggers: "run the hustle", "who's due", "follow-ups", or the morning skill. Airtable base `apprk3RUXdvZNJ8TF`, table `tblFy3hGmEDRBgExi`. Tick-boxes: Discovery Call `fldZsZog4AojVq4Gp`, Sales Call `fldJN2rVcM1V1GXc1`, Won `fldDkXfkfsu6HBXnn`.

**A. Pull the whole roster, not the due list.** Every row with Lead Status In Discussion, Engaged, Contacted, or New with a real source (not Kit Newsletter or Lead Magnet only). Drop Type Duplicate, Client family and Test. Page through every page of results. Follow-up Date is a hint, never a filter. Older rows have First and Last Name swapped. A lead on two rows is merged into the row with the most history, the other marked Type Duplicate, never deleted. Sort every row into one bucket:
- **LIVE:** any touch history (Last Action, Next Line, Follow-up Date), a Gmail thread or booking in the last 60 days, Temp Hot, or a draft in `scratch/`.
- **NURTURE:** Engaged or Warm with nothing else. No chase. Names only.
- **LAPSED:** Temp Lapsed or a lapsed Stage. Only the 4 to 6 week no-ask drip check.
Print a reconciliation line at the end: "N rows = LIVE a + NURTURE b + LAPSED c + duplicates d". A miss then shows up as a sum that doesn't add.

**B. Gather evidence for every LIVE lead, in this order.**
1. **Gmail**, both directions, 60 days, by the lead's address. Note who spoke last and when. Calendly confirmations arrive from notifications@calendly.com as "New Event", "Updated" or "Canceled".
2. **Google Calendar**, events titled "<Name> and Neil Taylor", 14 days back to 60 forward. Past means held, future means booked.
3. **Fathom** for held calls. 4. **Drive** (prospect or family folder, Sales Calls folder) and `scratch/` for drafts already written.
5. **WhatsApp and LinkedIn cannot be read by a connector.** Use Airtable's Last Action and anything Neil has pasted this session. For leads on those channels whose Last Action is over 3 days old, ask once, in one batched list, for screenshots. Never lead by lead.

**C. Sync status without being asked.**
- Booked call found: tick Discovery Call (Sales Call if it is a proposal call), Follow-up Date = the call date, Next Line "CALL <day, time UK>. Nothing to chase before it. After: post-call follow-up same day", Lead Status In Discussion.
- Held: keep the tick, Follow-up Date the next day. Canceled: untick, note, back on the ladder.
- A Family Onboarding call booked or a payment or invoice email: propose Won - Recent, never set it without Neil's yes.
- A reply received: log Last Action.
- A calendar reminder made obsolete by a booking: list it for Neil.

**D. Decide per lead, one line each:** stage (touch count from the evidence, days since last touch, moving or stalled, age), the rung (touch 1, touch 2, fork, exit, re-open, no-ask drip, call prep, post-call, or nothing today), relationship (close = friends, chummy = has met or a warm thread, unknown = stranger, from Knows Me plus the tone of the thread), channel, the one question, the tone in a few words. A lead with nothing due is listed as "no action, next <date>", never left out.

**E. Output.** One table: Lead | Relationship | Channel | Stage and days | Play | The one question | Due. Due today first. Then the drafts, grouped by channel, each with a suggested subject and two alternative hooks. No preamble.

**F. When Neil says "sent" or "actioned" for a lead:** log Last Action, its date and the next Follow-up Date in Airtable. Then write the NEXT rung's message, dated for 3 to 4 days later (touch 2) or 4 to 5 (fork). If the channel is Gmail, create it as a Gmail draft straight away, in the same thread. Create a Trello Inbox card due 9am UK on that date: name "<Lead>: <rung> via <channel>", description with the deadline, one line of context, the message text, the Gmail draft link if there is one, and the Airtable record id. Neil picks it up on the day, reviews and sends. Claude never sends.

Every draft still follows the ladder, voice rules, pricing rules and "Never" list in this file.

## A warm comment or reply becomes a DM (added 8 Oct 2026)

Comments on Neil's posts, or on a client's post that tags him, are the warmest cold-start signal he gets. Most deserve only a public reply. DM only when the person has said something about their own parent or asked, and no more than a handful per post.

**The three-signals test.** DM if the comment (1) names their own parent, (2) says they'll do it, or already did something with a parent, or (3) is a referral from the post's author. Reactions ("great idea", "lovely"), friends catching up and anyone with no parent in the comment get the public thread only.

**What each signal gets.**
- **Referred or asked** (e.g. Vincent Leonard, dad is 90, referred by the author): a lead from today. Public reply, then a DM the same day, one question about the parent plus a 20-minute offer. Start the ladder: touch 1 within 2 days, two real times from GCal and Calendly, touch 2 and the fork on the normal gaps. Create the Airtable row (ask for Lead Source, never pick one; Referred By is the post's author).
- **Their parent won't** (e.g. Chris Miller, "no way my old fella would do it"): not a lead. Ask what a mate would ask: is the other parent around? "Shame your old man won't go for it. Is your mum around? Could be worth asking her." If they reply, send the opt-in landing page for "How Do I Actually Bring This Up?" (written to the adult child, so it fits), never the raw Doc, and end the DM on one question.
- **Passed it to their parents** (e.g. Lex Scott): not a lead. A plain thank-you plus the question a friend would ask: "did it spark anything, are you thinking of doing something like it with them?" If they answer, offer a 20-minute chat and nothing more.

**The question must be one a normal person would ask.** Mirror what they actually said and ask about their real situation. Never a floating question designed to get engagement that the next message pounces on ("is there a story you've always wanted to hear?"). Neil, 8 Oct 2026: obvious bait. The model is the Vincent DM: "Are you thinking of having a go at recording him yourself, or would you rather someone else did the asking?" It sorts DIY from done-for-you and sounds like a person. If the lead said "borrow the idea", that may mean DIY, so ask which before offering any help.

**Rules.** Non-leads get one DM and no ladder until they put a hand up. A DM never carries a price, a deck or a lead magnet before the person has answered a question. The call is the only destination. Every DM draft comes with a suggested subject line (LinkedIn DMs have a subject field), written like a chapter title with the person's name and the real subject, e.g. "Sam, That Garage & Trying It Out". When the question touches something that might be sensitive (a parent who may have died or be unwell), Neil softens it with a self-aware aside ("I hope I'm not putting my foot in it here, but is your mum around?") and may add a short line of his own experience ("That's often been the way to make it happen with families"). Neil writes and sends every DM, never a VA, because his own voice is the asset. Put a Google Calendar reminder on every DM and ladder step with the drafted message in the description.

**Why.** Adviser input, untested with Neil: Hormozi's corpus (Jan to Oct 2026) says DMs are the warmest channel and the founder's own DM conversations outconvert a team's, and lists speed to contact and one conversion point as levers. His volume advice (10 to 20 cold DMs a day) does not apply, this is the opposite problem.

## Pricing the close, once a lead is moving (added 9 Oct 2026)

Order of concessions: ask for the sale at list first, then bonuses, then payment terms, then target price last. Never stack them. Each carries a reason and a decision date, and target is only ever offered against a decision by that date, never to break a silence. Bonuses before a discount because a discount teaches that the price moves (the books). A payment plan is the downsell, not the lead. Target and instalment figures come from `knowledge/offer/pricing.md`.

When the family has said yes on the call (parents opted in, buyer circling back), send the same day: a short deck with price, guarantee, the pay link and next steps, dated to the family's own timeline. Say nothing about bonuses, instalments or target. Send the deck as a PDF made with headless Chrome (see lessons.md, 9 Oct 2026), not a browser print. A Xero payment link raises a live invoice, so if a lever is used later, void that invoice first.

Invoices: search Xero for the surname first, copy the nearest existing draft, match its reference naming (Option, List or Target), set Account 200 - Sales, No VAT, and leave every invoice in Draft. Neil approves and sends.

## A new lead comes in

Neil says "add [name] to airtable, lead source [x], email, number". Create the row with Lead Source (dropdown), Knows Me if he says, Referred By for the referrer or publication, Email, Phone, Lead Status "In Discussion". If the source is missing, ask in one line. Never pick one. The Lead Source tab of the Lead Gen sheet is fed from these fields every Monday (see `knowledge/systems-map.md`). When a lead books or holds a discovery call or sales call, or wins, tick the box the same turn.

## What the advisers add

Hormozi: a deadline needs its own reason why, good selling feels good, price goes on a phone call. Donnelly: every follow-up gives something new, a graceful exit often gets the reply nothing else did, objections are interest. Neither teaches a post-call chase cadence in the current corpora, so the ladder is Neil's, built from the Daniel Beard line: "I've chased three times. If this isn't for you, just let me know, or let's get a date in the next two weeks." Never name him, or any adviser, in a message to a lead.

Gray (Instagram carousel, 2 Jul 2026, "invitation vs manipulation") backs the ladder rather than changing it: invite when they are ready, scarcity only if real (capacity or their own date), "not now" is an answer, never apologise for asking for money. The one addition is the last: say the price plainly on the call, no cushion before or after. Adviser input, not yet tested with Neil.

## Never

- Decide a lead is "too much to chase" from feel.
- Offer TARGET price to break a silence. TARGET is a landing zone offered for a decision by a date.
- Draft a fork for a lead who is moving.
- Write a closing line that claims a decision the other person is making.
- Quote an exact figure or TARGET in a message (a range, only when asked, is fine), or apologise for a number on the call.
- Send anything. Neil sends it.
