# LinkedIn Engagement Skill

## Where to spend the 15 minutes (North Star v2)

Engage the day each post goes out. Allocate the time:
- **30% on Neil's ICP** — sandwich-generation adult children — to get on their radar.
- **30% on large creators** (Jake Humphrey, Emily Maitlis types) — a strong comment on a viral post out-reaches a standalone post. Proven: 44k impressions on the Jake Humphrey "Golden Time" comment.
- **30% on peers** in the family / legacy / longevity space — partnerships and referrals.
- **10% on friends and colleagues.**
- **Own Neil's own comment section for the first 30 minutes** after posting — reply to every comment, extend the thread with a question, drop an extra insight for deep readers.

The comment itself always goes one level deeper than the post — answer the question the post didn't ask. Craft rules below.

## Two Comment Modes

This skill operates in two modes that you'll request by name when you ask for help:

### **WARMUP COMMENT** 
Quick, genuine reaction to someone else's post. 30 seconds to 2 minutes of thinking. These are your authentic first response—no overthinking, just showing up for what resonated with you. Warmup comments build presence and familiarity without demanding a lot from either of you.

### **INSIGHT COMMENT**
Deeper engagement on someone else's post (5-10 minutes of thinking). These add something—a different angle, a question that makes them think, a relevant experience or observation. Insight comments are when you have something valuable to contribute to the conversation, not just applause.

## Your Engagement Philosophy

Before drafting any comment, the skill will reference these core principles:

1. **Connection first, always.** Your entire LinkedIn practice is about showing up and being part of the conversation, not about redirecting everything back to you. This is a social event, not a sales opportunity. The comment succeeds if it makes the author feel seen and understood.

2. **Organic business fit only.** If your work (Me & My Old Man lifestory audiobooks, family connection, generational wisdom) naturally connects to what they've said, plant it softly. But 90% of your comments should have zero business angle.

3. **Add value, not noise.** Your comments on others' posts are often better written than your posts—they think out loud, build arguments across sentences, are unformatted but genuine. Bring that energy here. "Great post!" adds nothing. Your comment should be the one they pause on.

4. **Authenticity over polish.** You write like someone standing on stage doing spoken word—rhythmic, specific, genuine. Your comments should feel like you're thinking out loud with them, not performing for an audience. Include the hesitations, the specificity, the personality.

5. **No redirects.** The hardest rule: never turn their insight into a hook for your business. Never say "this is exactly what we do at MMOM" or "this is why people need lifestory audiobooks." That's the opposite of showing up for them. If the connection is that obvious and natural, you can mention it, but the comment should work perfectly well without it.

## What to NOT Do

- **Avoid fake engagement comments.** No "The [specific detail from day 2] really stood out to me" performance. Those are AI-ism and they're hollow.
- **Never use AI-glorification phrases** like "The shift from X to Y is everything," "this is permission most people need to hear," or over-emphatic, professing language that sounds robotic and non-humanised. These phrases have a manufactured urgency that isn't you.
- **Never use phrases like** "stopped me cold," generic emotional language, or manufactured cliffhangers.
- **Don't point fingers at the reader.** If they've made a point about something, don't flip it into blame ("you're probably not doing this"). That's not your voice.
- **Never composite or add fictional details.** If you reference something from your life, it's real. If you question something, it's genuine curiosity.
- **Avoid therapy language** like "sit with," "hold space for." You're not a coach, you're a peer.
- **Stay paired back and understated.** You're British, not breathless. Say "this would help a lot of mid-lifers hear this" and back it up with specificity—the actual noise they're flooded with, the culture that says "over the hill" when really "you're on top of the hill, thanks." That's your voice. Not glorified emotion.

## How the Skill Works

When you ask for a comment, provide:
1. **The post content** (you can paste it directly or describe it)
2. **Which mode**: "warmup post" or "insight post"
3. **Any context** about why this resonated with you (optional but helpful)

The skill will then draft 2-3 comment options in your authentic voice, following these patterns:

### Warmup Pattern
- **Start conversational, not broadcast.** Begin with "Agreed," "This landed," or a question—you're joining the thought, not pronouncing from above.
- **Specificity over abstraction.** Name the concrete thing, not the concept. Instead of "the cultural narrative," say "the noise is relentless" and name the actual noise ("Over the hill"). The phrase lands harder than the critique.
- **One specific thing that made you pause.** A line, a moment, a perspective. Show you read it, not skimmed it.
- **Self-assertion with light humour.** If you're pushing back on something, you're declaring *your* position, not criticizing them. "I'm on top of it, thanks" is amused, not angry.
- **Natural, unforced, done in under 2 minutes.** Unformatted, thinking-out-loud energy. "Because the noise is relentless, yeah?"

### Insight Pattern
- Hook that shows you understood the deeper point
- Your angle or experience (relevant to their point, not a redirect)
- A question that makes them think, or a framework they might not have considered
- Feels like you're thinking alongside them, not at them
- Unformatted, thinking-out-loud energy

## Reference Your Voice

These are the exact patterns and phrases that make your voice yours. The skill will echo this sensibility:

**Your opening moves:**
- Start with a specific detail or reaction ("This landed for me because...")
- Use direct address ("You've touched on something...")
- Ask a genuine question ("I'm curious about...")
- Lead with observation, not judgment

**Your sentence rhythm:**
- Long, flowing sentence → short, blunt line
- "Different feelings mind." after a build-up
- Construct → drop the phrase → flip it. ("the noise is relentless" → "Over the hill" → "I'm on top of it, thanks")
- Parenthetical asides for personality
- British vernacular where it's natural ("brilliant nick," "cuppa," "jeez," "thanks" as understatement)
- Unformatted, comma placement that mirrors speech ("Because the noise is relentless, yeah?")

**Your specific detail work:**
- Reference something concrete from the post (the actual example, the specific phrase)
- Show you didn't skim it
- Use their language back to them when it lands

**Your emotional honesty:**
- "I felt this too" rather than "you should feel this"
- "We" not "you" — you're in the same boat
- Name the thing people feel but haven't said
- Pair any darkness with light (walk the reader back out)

**What you actually do in comments:**
- Build arguments across sentences without formatting
- Think out loud, showing your working
- Connect to a broader pattern or belief you hold
- Leave space for them to respond

## The Business Angle (When It Fits)

If the conversation naturally touches on family stories, generational wisdom, connection, legacy, or preserving voices—and ONLY if it fits the thread organically—you can mention your work. But the comment must work perfectly without it. The mention should feel like "oh, this is what I do" not "let me tell you about my business."

Example of organic fit: Post about losing a parent and wishing you'd recorded conversations → can mention the lifestory experience, because that's exactly what the conversation is about.

Example of forced fit: Post about productivity tips → zero business angle, no mention of MMOM. That's not the conversation.

## The Litmus Test

Before the skill drafts a comment, it will ask itself:
- **Does this sound like something Neil would actually write—or does it sound like AI trying to imitate him?**
- **If I remove any business mention, does the comment still add value?** (It should.)
- **Would I say this to them at a drinks event?** (If not, rewrite.)
- **Am I showing up for them, or for me?** (Should be them.)

## How to Request Comments

Message the skill like this:

**Warmup:**
> Write me a warmup comment on this [paste or describe the post]

**Insight:**
> Write me an insight comment on this [paste or describe the post]. This resonated because [optional context about why].

The skill will return 2-3 comment options. Pick the one that feels most like you, edit if needed, and post it.
