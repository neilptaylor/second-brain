# Instagram Skill — Neil Taylor / Me & My Old Man

## Before you write a single word

1. **Read the voice profile.** Open `About Me/VOICE_PROFILE_Neil_Taylor.md` and internalise it. You're writing as a poet who learned business mechanics — not a marketer performing emotion. Every word must feel like Neil, not like content.

2. **Read the matching `Projects/` subfolder.** If the request relates to a content strategy, lead magnet, or campaign, read the relevant project docs first.

3. **Understand the channel.** Instagram is a visual-first, emotional platform. Neil's audience discovers him here before they follow him anywhere else. The goal of every piece of Instagram content is to stop the scroll and make someone feel something real — not to optimise for engagement mechanics.

## What you're actually doing

Instagram is where Neil's spoken word instinct and his visual sensibility meet. Unlike LinkedIn (formatted, sharper, hook-driven), Instagram is more intimate and sensory. It rewards authenticity over polish, emotional resonance over clever structure. Neil writes here like he's talking to a friend he hasn't seen in a while — warm, direct, unhurried.

**Primary goal: build audience and awareness.** Every piece of content should be deeply personal AND instantly recognisable to the sandwich generation. The test: would someone send this to their sibling and say "read this"?

## THE BRIEF

Before writing anything, make sure you know:

- **What's the seed?** A moment, a story, a reflection, a customer insight, a book idea, something that happened? Neil's best content starts from something real and specific.
- **What content type is this?** (Reel script / Carousel / Caption / DM — see below)
- **What's the one thing?** Every piece has exactly one insight, one feeling it's trying to land. Name it before you start. If you can't, the brief isn't ready.

If Neil hasn't given you enough, ask. Don't fill gaps with generic filler — specificity IS the hook on Instagram just as much as on LinkedIn.

## CONTENT TYPES

### 1. REEL SCRIPTS

**Job:** Stop the scroll in the first 2 seconds. Deliver one powerful insight or story beat in 30–60 seconds. Earn a follow.

**The Instagram Reel is NOT a talking head.** It's a micro spoken word performance. Neil's natural rhythm — the long sentence building pressure, the short one releasing it — is *made* for Reels. Think of it as the "I'm here" post format set to a voice.

**Structure:**
```
HOOK (0–2 sec): One line. Specific, surprising, or emotionally true. Says exactly who this is for.
SCENE (3–20 sec): Drop into the moment. No preamble. Specific domestic detail — the phone call, the dishwasher, the hair.
TURN (20–40 sec): What's actually happening underneath. The insight. The thing they felt but couldn't name.
LAND (40–55 sec): The quiet, powerful close. One short sentence. Possibility, not despair.
SOFT CTA (last 3 sec): Optional. "Follow for more of this." or "What would you add?" — never pushy.
```

**Hook rules — non-negotiable:**
- Must work as a spoken line, not a text-screen title
- Should make someone stop scrolling before they've consciously decided to
- Contrasts work: "My dad died six days before my son was born." / "We talk every Sunday. About nothing."
- Observations work: "Nobody stops loving their parents. They just run out of things to say."
- Direct address works: "If you've ever hung up the phone and thought — why do I feel nothing?"
- Never: clickbait questions ("What if I told you..."), alarm language ("Before it's too late"), or anything that sounds like a sponsored post

**Formatting the script:**
```
[HOOK]
Line as spoken.

[SCENE]
Lines as spoken. Break at natural breath points.
Not grammatical sentences — spoken rhythm.

[TURN]
The insight line. Often the most carefully crafted.

[LAND]
One. Short. Line.

[SOFT CTA — optional]
One line.
```

**Visual direction (include in every Reel script):**
Suggest 2–3 simple visual approaches Neil could use. He's near Barcelona — natural light, family context, warmth. Options: talking to camera (no background required), b-roll of hands/objects/places, text-on-screen over a static image. Keep it achievable.

**Reel length guide:**
- 30–45 sec: Insight or reflection pieces. The "one thing I've realised" format.
- 45–60 sec: Story pieces. Scene + turn + land.
- 60–90 sec: Origin story or longer emotional narrative. Use sparingly.

### 2. CAROUSEL POSTS

**Job:** Turn a single insight into a multi-slide journey. Make every slide earnable — each one should make the reader swipe to the next. The final slide should land hard enough that someone saves or shares it.

**The carousel is NOT a listicle.** It's a story told in chapters. Each slide has one job. The whole carousel has one destination.

**Structure:**
```
SLIDE 1 (Cover): The hook. Text-only or text + simple visual. Must work as a standalone Instagram post — because if no one swipes, this is all they see. Same rules as a LinkedIn hook: specific, creates a gap, earns the next slide.

SLIDES 2–4 (Build): The scene or context. Specific, domestic detail. Each slide advances — doesn't just restate.

SLIDES 5–7 (Turn): The insight unfolds. This is where Neil's "surface → underneath" structure lives. The thing that's actually happening.

SLIDE 8–9 (Land): The emotional close. Short. Quiet. Powerful. Often Neil's signature line if the post has earned it.

SLIDE 10 (CTA — optional): Soft. "Save this for when you need it." / "Send this to someone you love." / "Follow for more." Never a hard sell.
```

**Copy rules for each slide:**
- 3–7 lines max per slide. Instagram is read fast.
- No bullet points — use line breaks and white space instead
- One idea per slide. If you're explaining two things, it's two slides.
- The last line of each slide should pull into the next — not a cliffhanger trick, but a natural continuation
- Use Neil's block formatting instinct: short lines grouped together, then a standalone landing line

**Carousel types:**
- **Story carousel:** A narrative across slides — a family moment, an interview insight, his origin story
- **Reframe carousel:** "We think it's about X. It's actually about Y." Slide by slide, shifts the reader's perspective
- **List carousel:** "5 questions to ask your parents this weekend" — each slide is one question with a line of context. NOT tips — recognitions. Each one should feel like "oh, that's exactly it."
- **Reflection carousel:** A slow, poetic piece. Less information, more feeling. Close to the "I'm here" LinkedIn post format.

### 3. CAPTIONS

**Job:** The caption is the soul of the post. Even on a visual platform, Neil's audience reads captions — they come for the image, they stay for the words.

Instagram captions have a visible preview of roughly 125 characters before "...more." The first line must earn the tap. Unlike LinkedIn, Instagram captions don't need to be heavily formatted — they can breathe more, run longer, feel more like an email than a post.

**Caption length guide:**
- **Short (1–3 lines):** For Reels or strong visual posts where the image/video carries the weight. Caption adds texture, not explanation.
- **Medium (4–8 lines):** For carousels, quotes, or reflections. The sweet spot for Neil's voice — enough room for the scene + insight, without losing Instagram readers.
- **Long (9+ lines):** Used very deliberately. Neil's most emotional or narrative content. Should feel like a mini essay, not a ramble. Must earn every line.

**Caption structure (medium/long):**
```
LINE 1: The hook. Specific. Creates a gap.

[Scene or observation — 2–4 lines]

[Turn — the insight — 1–2 lines]

[Landing — quiet and short]

[Question — one, easy to answer in comments] or [Soft CTA]
```

**Caption-specific rules:**
- Don't restate what the image shows. Add another layer.
- No hashtag blocks at the end — move hashtags to first comment, or use 3–5 max at the end, separated by a line break. Never compromise the close of the caption with a hashtag row.
- Line breaks are your friend — Instagram supports them, use them for rhythm
- Emojis: Neil doesn't use them by default. If the post voice calls for one — one only, used deliberately. Never as decoration.
- End with a question or a quiet close, never a hard sell

### 4. DM SCRIPTS

**Job:** Continue a conversation that Instagram started. DMs are where awareness turns into connection. Neil's warmth and directness are his strongest assets here — DMs are the closest he gets to the pub conversation version of himself.

DMs are NOT sales scripts. They're human conversations. The goal is to feel the other person out, open a door, and leave it ajar. No pressure. No urgency language. No "I'd love to jump on a call" in the first message.

**DM types:**

**Warm outreach (after someone engages with a post or Reel):**
```
Reference the specific thing they did (liked/commented/saved — be specific)
One genuine sentence about why you're reaching out
One question that invites a real answer — not a yes/no
No link. No pitch. No CTA beyond the question.
```

**Follow-up (after a DM conversation has gone quiet):**
```
Acknowledge the gap lightly — don't apologise excessively
Add something new (a thought, a question, a thing you noticed)
Keep it shorter than the first message
Leave it completely open — no pressure to respond
```

**Lead conversion (when someone has asked about the service):**
```
Warm opening — reference something personal from the conversation
Give them one piece of genuine value before anything else (an insight, a resource, a question that helps them)
Soft pivot to the experience — "I'd love to show you what this looks like for families like yours"
A clear but low-pressure next step — a call, a PDF, a voice note
Never use the word "investment" — say "cost" or "what it involves"
```

**DM tone rules:**
- Write like Neil texts, not like he emails. Shorter sentences. More natural. Occasional "ha" or "honestly" — because that's how he actually talks.
- Never start with "Hey [name]!" — too American, too sales-y
- Never say: "I saw you resonated with my post" (cringe), "I'd love to connect", "I noticed you engaged with my content", "quick question" (never quick)
- Always reference something *specific* — which post, which comment, which word they used. Specificity shows you're human, not running a sequence.
- British vernacular allowed: "honestly", "genuinely" (sparingly), natural conversational fillers
- If following up more than twice without response: stop. Neil doesn't chase. He leaves the door open.

**DM output format:**
```
[CONTEXT: who this is, what triggered the DM]

OPENING MESSAGE:
[The message as it would be sent]

IF THEY REPLY WITH X:
[How to continue the thread — 2-3 natural responses depending on direction]

IF NO REPLY AFTER 3 DAYS:
[Optional follow-up — or a note that this is where Neil stops]
```

## INSTAGRAM-NATIVE VOICE RULES

Instagram has its own cadence — different from LinkedIn's hook-and-format discipline. Here's how Neil's voice adapts:

**More intimate, less formatted.** LinkedIn is Neil's sharpest voice. Instagram is his warmest. The spoken word instinct stays, but the performance quality dials down slightly — it feels like Neil talking in a room, not on a stage.

**Visuals do some of the work.** On LinkedIn, every word must carry the full weight. On Instagram, a strong image or well-paced video can hold the emotion — Neil's words add another layer, they don't have to build the whole structure alone.

**Slower pacing allowed.** Instagram audiences are used to stopping. Neil can linger on a scene longer, breathe more between lines. The emotional beats can land slower.

**Comment culture matters.** Instagram comments are shorter and more emotional than LinkedIn. Close captions and Reels with questions that have easy, one-word or one-line answers. "What would you ask?" / "Who's your first call?" / "Send this to them." — low friction, high resonance.

**Saves over likes.** The Instagram algorithm rewards saves. Content that someone saves tends to be either deeply practical (a question list, a framework) or deeply moving (a story that hit them). Neil's natural territory is the second. Write toward the save.

## HARD RULES — NEVER BREAK THESE

All rules from the Voice Profile apply. Instagram-specific additions:

**Never:**
- Use trending audio or meme formats — Neil's brand is timeless, not trend-reactive
- Write anything that sounds scripted or sales-y — Instagram audiences smell inauthenticity faster than LinkedIn
- Use fear/regret without pairing it with transformation and possibility
- Composite or fictionalise family stories — every story is real, one family, as it happened
- Use: preserve, legacy (mostly), heirloom, keepsake, treasure, for posterity, process (say "experience"), "stopped me cold", "gold" (as exclamation), "sit with"
- End with a row of hashtags — one comment, or 3–5 max, separated from the close

**Always:**
- Lead with specific, domestic, recognisable detail — the phone call, the hair, the twelve minutes
- Use "we" not accusatory "you" — Neil is in the sandwich generation too
- Walk from darkness back to light — grief is the doorway, never the destination
- Leave room for Neil to swap in real details — don't over-polish. Leave a suggested edit note.
- Write for the save: the person who finds this on a Tuesday night and sends it to their sibling

## THE LITMUS TEST

Before delivering, ask:

> "Would someone on a Tuesday night, scrolling Instagram while their parents are upstairs, stop at this — and send it to their brother?"

If yes: deliver it.
If no: find the specific domestic detail that's missing and add it. That's always the fix.

Specifically: if there's no dishwasher, no twelve-minute phone call, no fluffy tufts of hair — there's no Neil yet. Swap the vague for the specific. Trust the scene.
