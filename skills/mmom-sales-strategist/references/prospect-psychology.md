# Prospect Psychology Reference

## Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

**Core profile:** Sarah, 35–50, "sandwich generation family connector"
- Managing young children AND aging parents simultaneously
- Time-poor but relationship-centred
- Emotionally responsible for preserving family connection
- Triggered by milestones or health scares
- Seeks a meaningful done-for-you solution

**Demographics sweet spot:** Age 42, suburban/metro, household income £60–120k, 2–3 kids aged 8–16, parents 70+

**Psychographics:**
- Self-identity: Family's emotional CEO — the organiser, rememberer, holder of traditions
- Core values: Connection over convenience, meaning over materialism
- Dominant feelings: Guilt, urgency, overwhelm, love, responsibility
- Secret fear: "What if Mum dies and I never asked her about her life?"
- Internal dialogue: "I should do this… but when? How? What if I mess it up?"
- Aspiration: To be remembered as the one who held the family together

## The 5 Fears (Ranked by Intensity)

### Fear 1: The Ticking Clock (10/10)
Watches parents age knowing every day is another story lost.
- Key language: "Running out of time," "Before it's too late," "It happened overnight"
- Trigger: "6–9 months from fine to full dementia"
- Content hook: "She was basically fine 14 months ago. Now she doesn't recognise me."

### Fear 2: The DIY Trap (9/10)
Bought a journal, downloaded an app, started recording — all abandoned.
- Key language: "She never wrote a single story," "I lost EVERYTHING," "Good intentions aren't enough"
- StoryWorth failure is the perfect proof point
- Content hook: "Nothing I've tried has worked"

### Fear 3: The Conversation Barrier (8/10)
Doesn't know how to start deep conversations without it feeling forced or morbid.
- Fear: "Will Dad think I'm planning his funeral?"
- Reality: Parents WANT to share — they just need the right invitation
- Key language: "We don't have those kinds of conversations"

### Fear 4: The Regret (10/10)
The highest-intensity fear. Future regret for not acting.
- Key language: "I wish I had asked…" (48 instances in research), "I should have asked…" (23 instances)
- Most potent quotes: "NEVER pass up a chance to tap their memories. Once they're gone — it's too late."
- Content hook: "The stories I'll never hear"

### Fear 5: The Disconnection (7/10)
Children growing up without knowing where they come from.
- Key language: "Who they were before you knew them," "The threads of your life started long before you were born"
- Three-generation wish: wants kids to have relationship with grandparents' stories

## Trigger Moments (When She's Most Buyable)

**High-intent:**
- Parent's milestone birthday (60th, 70th, 75th, 80th)
- Health scare or hospitalisation (3–6 week window post-event)
- Death of a parent's friend or sibling (mortality reminder)
- Inheritance/estate planning conversation

**Calendar triggers:**
- Father's Day / Mother's Day (6–8 weeks before)
- Christmas (Oct–Dec gift-seeking mode)
- Anniversary milestones (50th wedding anniversary, etc.)

**Life stage triggers:**
- Own child's milestone (graduation, wedding)
- Moving parent to assisted living
- Parent's retirement

## Decision Journey

**Discovery (Weeks 1–2):** Triggered by event → search → discovers MMOM → consumes content

**Consideration (Weeks 2–4):** Compares options → discusses with spouse → calculates mental ROI

**Decision (Days–1 week):** Needs final push: scarcity, testimonial, founder story. Often decides emotionally, justifies logically. May need payment plan to overcome spouse resistance.

**Post-purchase:** High engagement, vocal advocate if positive, will refer friends with similar triggers.

---

## Multi-Sibling Dynamics

The lead prospect is often "the one who always initiates." Siblings:
- May not contribute financially but will judge if it's not done
- Create a race to the bottom if given tiered options
- Need social proof (testimonials) not pitching
- Respond better to being "invited in" than sold to

**Strategy:** Get all siblings aligned early. One clear proposal. Let the lead prospect be the hero. Offer to jump on a call with reluctant siblings directly.
