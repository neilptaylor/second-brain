# MMOM Brand & Voice Reference

## Brand Essence

**Tagline:** Stories That Matter. With People You Love.

**Purpose:** Bring families closer — now — by creating warm, guided spaces where life stories are spoken, heard, and felt together.

**The human need served:** Love & Belonging (Maslow). Not preservation. Not heritage. Present-moment connection.

**Key reframe:** This isn't about the past. It's about connecting NOW — while parents are alive, voices are intact, relationships can deepen.

---

## Voice Principles

1. **Closeness over cleverness** — If a line doesn't deepen connection, delete it
2. **Plain language** — No jargon, no corporate speak
3. **Invite, include, affirm** — "We" not "you vs. them"
4. **Present-moment focus** — Connection now, not preservation for later
5. **Less polish, more presence** — Conversation, not performance

## Tone in Practice

- Warm & reassuring: "This isn't a session to rush — give it the space it deserves."
- Reflective & grounded: "What I would give to hear his voice again."
- Clear & calm: "No prep. No pressure. Just presence."
- Emotionally intelligent: acknowledge without amplifying

---

## Banned Words — NEVER USE

| Banned | Use instead |
|--------|-------------|
| Heirloom | Stories, recordings, audiobook |
| Keepsake | Memento, audio chapter |
| Preserve | Capture, create, record |
| Legacy | Story, history, voice |
| Gift of voice | Their voice, in their words |
| Heavy lifting | (just avoid entirely) |
| Precious | (too saccharine) |
| Priceless | (too cliché) |

## Five Messaging Pillars

1. **"Before It's Too Late"** — Gentle urgency rooted in real regret and real opportunity
2. **"Not Just for the Future, But for Now"** — About bonding, reflection, and healing today
3. **"Let Them Be Heard, Truly Heard"** — Not just recorded — witnessed, received, understood
4. **"A Gift That Grows Over Time"** — Deepens with every listen
5. **"We Make It Easy. You Make It Meaningful."** — Remove friction; guided approach

## 7-Point Brand QC Checklist

Before publishing anything:
1. **Closeness test:** Does this increase love, belonging, or connection?
2. **Plain English:** Jargon removed?
3. **Banned terms:** No preserve/legacy/heirloom/keepsake?
4. **Action bias:** Does the CTA invite listening together before purchase?
5. **Question quality:** Are prompts concrete and story-opening?
6. **Tone audit:** Warm, reflective, with positive/active energy?
7. **"We" not "you":** Does it create shared experience?

## Content Authenticity Tests

**The Sarah Test:** Would Sarah (busy, guilt-ridden, time-poor) feel this was written specifically for her, or for a generic audience?

**The Cringe Test:** Does this sound like something a life coach would post? If yes, rewrite.

**The Verbatim Test:** Could you drop this into a Reddit thread about aging parents and have people think it was written BY someone in the sandwich generation, not TO them?

**The Action Test:** Does this make someone want to call their parent right now, book a call with Neil, or forward to a sibling?

**The Specificity Test:** Is there a SPECIFIC detail (6–9 months, StoryWorth failure, Stephen's dad's dream) or is it vague? Specific = real.

---

## Hook Creation Formula

**HIGH-PERFORMING HOOKS:**
[SPECIFIC TIME/SITUATION] + [EMOTIONAL REALITY] + [SURPRISING DETAIL]

**Good examples:**
- "6–9 months. That's how long it took my client's mum to go from 'a bit forgetful' to not recognising her daughter. The stories? Gone."
- "She bought her dad StoryWorth for Christmas. £100. Two years later: zero stories written. He died last month."
- "My mate's dad told me about a dream about his grandfather. When I asked if he'd heard it before, he said no. They talk every week."

**Bad examples:**
- "Preserve your parent's legacy before it's too late." (banned word, no specificity)
- "Family stories are precious gifts." (banned word, no urgency)
- "3 Reasons to Record Your Parents' Stories Today" (listicle, no emotion)

## The "Stephen's Dad Dream" Principle

Neil's signature story: After 4 hours of interviews with Stephen's dad, he shared a spontaneous dream about his grandfather. Neil called Stephen: "Did you know about that story?" Stephen: "No, never heard it." Neil: "You NEVER would have heard that — because you talk about sport and golf and skiing."

**Why it works:** Specific time + surprising reveal + proof of hidden depth + Neil's expertise + universality ("everyone has unheard stories")

**Use for:** Overcoming "we're already close" objection; showing value of professional facilitation; sales calls; content hooks about hidden stories.
