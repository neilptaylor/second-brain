# Funnel Roles — TOFU vs MOFU

A lead magnet is not a single thing — its job depends on where the reader is in the funnel. The voice stays the same. The calibration changes.

---

## Top-of-Funnel (Cold → Email List)

**Who's reading:** Someone who just saw a LinkedIn post or Instagram reel. They don't know Neil. They gave up an email address because the promise was concrete and the friction was low. They owe him nothing. One whiff of a sales pitch and they're gone.

**Job of the asset:** Deliver so much obvious utility that the reader thinks *"who is this guy — I want more of him"* by the end.

**CTA calibration:** Very soft. One of:
- *"Reply and tell me what came up."*
- *"If this landed, the next email from me will be…"* (and then actually make the next email land)
- *"Listen to the conversation this came from →"* (Terry Podcast or a specific Drift email)

**NEVER in a TOFU asset:** the Discovery Call link. Never. You haven't earned it. The asset's job is to make the reader open email #2. That's it.

**Length of soft-sell at the end:** 3-5 lines. Not a page.

**Tone:** Generous. Specific. A little vulnerable. Not hungry.

**Example ending:**

> *You don't need to do anything with this guide right now. Put it in your notes. The next time you're about to say 'how was your day' on a phone call with your mum, pull up one of these questions instead.*
>
> *And if something surfaces when you do — hit reply. I read everything.*

---

## Mid-Funnel (Nurture Existing List)

**Who's reading:** Someone already on the Stories That Matter list. They've seen Neil's voice. They trust him a little. They've maybe opened six of his emails. This asset is a reward for that attention — and a deepening.

**Job of the asset:** Move the reader one degree closer to understanding what the MMOM experience actually is. Give them the inside view. Make the house-and-rooms metaphor land. Earn the Discovery Call without asking for it yet.

**CTA calibration:** Warmer. One of:
- *"This is a preview of the kind of conversation we have in the 12-week experience. If you want to see what that looks like, [book a no-pressure chat]."*
- *"I've built a free 15-minute Discovery Call for exactly this. Here's the link →"*
- *"Listen to the full 60-minute Terry episode where we went into this deeper →"*

**Length of CTA:** Still short. 4-6 lines. Still not a sales page.

**Tone:** Less introductory, more collegial. The reader is in the room now.

**Example ending:**

> *This is the part of my work that doesn't fit in a LinkedIn post. It takes time, and a specific kind of attention, and a structure that holds you while it does its thing.*
>
> *If you've been feeling the drift — and if this guide named something you already half-knew — then the next step might be a 15-minute conversation with me. No pitch. Just a chat about where things are in your family.*
>
> *[Book a Discovery Call →]*

---

## Which Role, Practically

- If Neil is driving traffic from LinkedIn / IG / a podcast / a guest post / a cold newsletter feature → **TOFU**.
- If Neil is sending the lead magnet to his existing Stories That Matter list → **MOFU**.
- If Neil is handing it to warm leads after a Discovery Call as a "thanks for the chat, here's more" → **MOFU**, and you can add one extra line pointing to the sales page.

---

## What Never Changes Between Roles

- The voice.
- The specificity.
- The quality of the utility.
- The closing reflection (always a quiet, standalone line — never a high-pressure tee-up).
- The P.S. in the delivery email — always carries a piece of Neil's own story.
