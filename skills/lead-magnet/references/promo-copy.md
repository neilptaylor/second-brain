# Promo Copy — Landing Page, Delivery Email, Three-Platform Posts

Every lead magnet ships with the distribution machinery attached. If Neil has to write any of this himself on top of the asset, the skill hasn't done its job.

---

## 1. Landing Page (One Screen)

The landing page is the first trust test. Don't overwrite it. The best MMOM landing pages are one scroll, high-signal, and sound like Neil wrote them in ten minutes.

**Structure:**

```
[HEADLINE]
One clear promise. No cleverness.
Max 10 words. Sentence case.

[SUBHEAD]
One sentence that adds the specific detail or named pain.
Max 20 words.

[WHAT'S INSIDE — 3-5 bullets]
- Concrete things, not benefits.
- "10 questions, grouped into warm-up, middle depth, and the door you've never opened."
- Not: "A framework to unlock deeper family connection."

[WHO IT'S FOR — 1 line]
"For the adult child who feels the drift and doesn't know how to talk about it."

[EMAIL CAPTURE]
One field: email. One button: "Send it to me".
Not "Download". Not "Get Instant Access". Not "Claim Your Free Guide".

[REASSURANCE LINE]
"No spam. One email a week. Unsubscribe any time."

[ABOUT NEIL — 50 words max, optional]
"I'm Neil. I started Me & My Old Man after my dad died and I realised I'd never really asked him his story. I now help families do what I didn't. This guide is a small taste of that work."
```

**Voice rules for landing page:**
- British restraint over American hype.
- No emojis in headlines.
- No countdown timers, no "limited time", no false scarcity.
- No fake testimonials. If you have real ones from Neil, use them. Otherwise, skip the section.

## 2. Delivery Email (Sent the Moment They Opt In)

The delivery email has three jobs:

1. Confirm the download actually landed (link to the PDF).
2. Add one small, unexpected piece of bonus value (an extra question, a short reflection, a behind-the-scenes note).
3. Invite a reply.

**Template:**

```
Subject: [sentence case, 3-7 words, specific — "Your questions are inside" is too generic; "Here's your 10 questions, Sarah" is too name-insert; "That guide you asked for" is the right temperature]

Preview: [one line that works on its own, doesn't repeat subject]

---

[HOOK — 1-2 lines of specific detail]
"Here's the PDF I promised you. I hope it does something."

[LINK — above the fold, styled as a link, not a button]
→ [Title of Guide] (PDF)

[BONUS — 3-5 lines]
One extra question, or one extra reflection, or a line from a session that didn't make it into the guide. This is the unexpected gift.

[INVITATION — 2-3 lines]
"When something surfaces, hit reply and tell me what came up. I read everything."

[SIGNOFF]
Neil

[P.S. — MAX 2 LINES, always clickable]
"P.S. The story behind this guide is in episode 4 of the Terry Podcast. If you want the long version → [link]"
```

**Voice rules for delivery email:**
- The P.S. is prime real estate. Put Neil's most specific, vulnerable, or personal line there.
- Never call the reader "friend" or use "Hey [name]". Neil's voice doesn't do that.
- Don't ask for a social share in the delivery email. Too early. You haven't earned it.

## 3. Promo Posts — LinkedIn, Instagram, Newsletter

Three short pieces of copy, each calibrated to the platform.

### LinkedIn Post (150-250 words)

Follow the Matt Gray structural discipline that Neil uses for LinkedIn — but inject Neil's own voice, not generic hook-engineered content.

```
[HOOK — 1-2 short lines, personal, specific. Start with "I", "Last week", or a direct observation.]

[NAMED PAIN — 3-5 lines, one thought per line, short paragraphs]
The drift. The 'how was your day, fine'. The thing the reader half-knows.

[THE MOMENT / REFRAME — 3-4 lines]
What shifted for Neil, or what shifted for a family he worked with.

[THE OFFER — 2-3 lines]
"I've put this into a guide. [Title]. Ten questions, free, no pitch."

[LINK / CTA]
"Link to the guide in the comments."

[CLOSING QUESTION — 1 line]
"What's the one question you haven't asked your parent yet?"
```

**Voice rules for LinkedIn:**
- No emojis in the opener.
- No "here's the truth about [topic]" templated openers (Neil has that tic already — the skill should reduce it, not reinforce it).
- Never put the link in the post body. Always "link in comments".
- Closing question should be specific and sayable, not abstract.

### Instagram Caption (80-150 words)

Softer. More image-led. Works alongside a visual pull-quote from the PDF.

```
[ONE-LINE HOOK — vivid, lands without setup]

[2-3 SHORT PARAGRAPHS]
Name the feeling. Name the drift. Keep it warm.

[THE OFFER — 1-2 lines]
"New free guide. [Title]. Link in bio."

[CLOSING LINE — 1 line, quiet]
A small reflection or a single question.
```

**Voice rules for Instagram:**
- You can use one, maybe two, emojis here. Use sparingly and only if they add feeling (🤍 works; 🔥 doesn't).
- No hashtag stacks. 3-5 tasteful tags at most.
- Carousels outperform single images — if the PDF supports it, suggest pulling 4-6 key quotes as a carousel with a "link in bio for the full guide" final slide.

### Newsletter Blurb (75-125 words)

For the Stories That Matter list. Goes at the bottom of an existing newsletter or as a standalone drop.

```
[ONE-SENTENCE INTRO]
"A quick thing I wanted to share."

[2-3 LINES OF WHAT THIS IS]
Plain, direct. What's in the guide. Why Neil made it now.

[THE LINK — inline, not a button]
"→ [Title of guide] (free, 10-minute read)"

[ONE-LINE CLOSE]
"If it lands, hit reply."
```

**Voice rules for newsletter blurb:**
- This is the audience that knows Neil best. Write to them like friends.
- Don't repeat yourself if the lead magnet topic was already covered in a recent email. Add the "why this is a distilled version" angle.
- No subject line — this blurb lives inside another email.

## 4. Consistency Check

Before saving all three promo posts, run this check:

- [ ] Same promise across all three (no platform has a stronger claim than the others — be honest).
- [ ] Same named pain, described in the specific voice of that platform.
- [ ] One reader could see all three and not feel contradicted.
- [ ] Each promo has exactly one CTA. No stacking.
- [ ] Link destinations are consistent (all land on the same landing page URL).

If any box fails — rewrite before handing over.
