## What the data actually says (Kit, last 90 days, checked 4 Aug 2026)

Three findings. Use these to settle any dispute with the older guidance below.

**Opens are already excellent — the problem is clicks, and it's concentrated in one email type.** Account-wide open rate is 53.6%, well above Lara's "30-50% is good" benchmark and above most personal-brand averages. Click rate sits at 2.6%. The two best-performing individual emails this year were craft/process pieces — "Why I never start with 'how did that make you feel?'" (8.47% CTR, 116 clicks) and "The Sunday I wish I'd pressed record" (7.66% CTR, 114 clicks) — both Behind the Mic pillar, both paired with a subject line that promises an insight rather than a story. Meanwhile several pure Transformation/story emails landed at 0% clicks despite 53-58% opens: "He came to tell me about his life," "What most dads actually want for Father's Day," "I didn't know that about him," "He thought it would be a couple of hours." **The pattern: story-only emails earn the open but the go-deeper doesn't land — either it's genuinely bolted on, or a story-only email isn't the right vehicle for a click at all.** When writing a pure Transformation email, don't force the same go-deeper mechanic used elsewhere; consider whether presence alone (no click ask) is the honest job for that email.

**List growth is close to flat — this is a top-of-funnel problem, not a writing problem.** 246 subscribers, only 7 net new in 90 days (33 joined, 26 cancelled). The newsletter is retaining and engaging the people already on it; it isn't bringing new people in. That's not something a better email fixes — it's downstream of how many people see a Lead-gen LinkedIn post and comment for the resource. Keep the LinkedIn Lead-gen cadence (one/week) protected; it's now doing double duty as the newsletter's growth engine, not just LinkedIn's.

**Vulnerability + specificity is still the single best-performing combination.** The highest all-time open/click pairing in the account is "I've been too scared to send this for 11 months" (75.3% open, 16.85% CTR). This matches the LinkedIn analytics finding almost exactly — personal, specific, slightly risky material outperforms polished or seasonal content in both channels.

## THE BUSINESS PURPOSE — NEVER FORGET THIS

The newsletter is **not entertainment**. It has a commercial role.

Every email must do three things:

1. **Build trust** — give something genuinely worth reading, no purchase required
2. **Build credibility** — demonstrate depth, real family stories, lived experience
3. **Move one step further** — via a single, natural, low-friction go-deeper offer

**A go-deeper element is non-negotiable in every email.** Not a hard sell. Not a command. A door, clearly labelled, standing open at the end of an email worth reading.

### The key metrics

- **Open rate** — driven by subject line quality and sender trust. Track every send. A falling open rate is an early warning. Fix the subject line first, then check send frequency.
- **Click rate** — driven by the go-deeper offer and how naturally it flows from the email. Low click rate = the offer didn't earn the click, or it didn't feel like the right next step for that email's emotional arc.
- **Reply rate** (secondary) — high reply rate = deep resonance. "Hit reply and tell me one thing" is the invite. Replies are the proof of trust.

When drafting: write the subject line with open rate in mind. Write the go-deeper with click rate in mind. Write the body with reply rate in mind.

### Go-deeper options (rotate — choose the one that fits the email's emotional logic)

| Option | When it fits |
|--------|-------------|
| **Lead magnet** (107 questions) | Any email about what to ask / voice loss / where to start / the parent who thinks they have nothing to say |
| **Recent podcast appearance** | When the episode covers the same theme — link with one line of context |
| **A specific LinkedIn post** | When the email references content Neil has already published |
| **A Reel or clip** | When there's a short video that deepens the same insight |
| **Discovery call** | When the email lands on transformation / readiness / "I want this for my family" |
| **Waiting list** | When capacity is limited or a new cohort is coming |

### How to write the go-deeper so it doesn't feel bolted on

It must grow from the email's closing line — not arrive as a separate sales note.

> **Email ends on "you still have time" → lead magnet is the first step:**
> "If you want to start — I made a list of 107 questions to ask your parent while you still can. They're free: [link]"

> **Email ends on transformation → discovery call is the natural door:**
> "If you want this for your family, the way to start is a 30-minute conversation. Calendar here: [link]"

> **Email references a specific post or Reel:**
> "I posted about this on LinkedIn last week — the comments thread is worth a look: [link]"

**Position:** after the body has landed, before the sign-off. One sentence or two. Link-bearing. Never a header. Never a bullet list of benefits.

**Never:** put it in the opening or middle of the email. Never repeat it twice. Never make it the subject of the email — it's the last thing said, not the reason for writing.

## The LinkedIn-to-Newsletter handoff

The most natural creative flow: a LinkedIn post sparks the newsletter.

**How it works:**
- A LinkedIn post (Pillar 1–4) lands well — it names something, earns replies, resonates
- That post is the seed. The newsletter takes the same truth and goes deeper
- LinkedIn is the hook. The newsletter is the room you walk into after
- The go-deeper offer in the newsletter can loop back to that LinkedIn post, a Reel, or a podcast that covers the same ground

**Repurposing, as a concrete step (do this before writing from scratch):** When Neil is short an idea for the week's email, check the last 1-2 weeks of LinkedIn posts for the one that earned the most saves/comments/reposts. That post is the seed — don't reuse it verbatim. LinkedIn is the idea compressed for scroll; the newsletter unfolds the same truth across mixed paragraph lengths, with room to earn the reader before asking anything of them. This is the single fastest route to a strong email when the well feels dry, and it means most weeks don't require a new idea, just an unfolding of one that already proved itself.

**The key shift:**
- LinkedIn: one idea, compressed, formatted for scroll
- Newsletter: one story, unfolded, formatted for reading
- LinkedIn closes with a hard CTA (Save / Repost / Comment / Follow). Newsletter closes with a soft go-deeper — a door, not a command.

**What doesn't transfer:**
- The one-line paragraph format. Newsletter uses mixed paragraph lengths.
- The hard CTA mechanics. Newsletter CTAs are softer: a link, a question, a gentle invite.
- The hook compression. Newsletter can earn the reader across 600 words before asking for anything.

## The three modes

### MODE 1: SINGLE WEEKLY EMAIL

One email. Standalone. Part of ongoing trust-building.

**Purpose:** Maintain presence. Deepen connection. Give something worth reading. Always leave one clear door open.

**Structure (the natural arc):**
1. **Scene** — a specific, domestic, recognisable moment. Never an abstraction. Always the thing itself.
2. **Underneath** — what's actually going on beneath the surface. The drift. The thing unsaid.
3. **Try this** (optional) — one small, specific action. One human thing.
4. **Go deeper** — one natural, earned offer. See above. Position just before the sign-off.
5. **Tease** (optional) — a single line pointing toward next week's email.
6. **P.S.** — the most important moment in the email. Neil's most vulnerable material lives here. Not a sales hook — the quiet thing said after stepping back from the mic.

**Format:**
- Subject line: specific, domestic, slightly unfinished. "We talk about the dishwasher." Not: "5 ways to connect with your parent."
- Preview text: completes or deepens the subject. Under 90 characters.
- Opening: "Hey," — always lowercase, always a comma
- Body: mixed paragraph lengths. Long builds pressure, short drops the floor.
- Go deeper: brief, natural, after the body close and before "Neil". One or two lines with the link.
- Sign off: "Neil" — just that.
- P.S.: always. Carries the emotional weight.

### MODE 2: WAVE (MULTI-EMAIL SERIES)

A themed sequence of 4–6 emails with a shared through-line. Each stands alone but accumulates meaning. The Drift series is the gold standard.

**What makes a wave work:**
- One coherent theme, named precisely
- Emotional escalation: Email 1 names the problem softly. Email 5 earns the invitation to act.
- Go-deeper offers escalate across the wave: Emails 1–3 → lead magnet / content. Emails 4–5 → discovery call / waiting list.
- The CTA doesn't arrive fully until the arc has been earned.

**Wave architecture:**

| Email | Job | Emotional note | Go deeper |
|-------|-----|---------------|-----------|
| 1 | Name the thing | "That's my family" | Lead magnet or related content |
| 2 | Reframe the thing | "I hadn't seen it that way" | Lead magnet or podcast |
| 3 | Show what's underneath | "I wonder if my parents..." | Related post or Reel |
| 4 | Unexpected dimension | "I didn't see that coming" | Podcast or related content |
| 5 | The invitation | "Someone has to go first" | Discovery call, warm and clear |
| Bridge | Transition | Exhale | Waiting list or soft re-invite |

**Spacing:** Day 0 (welcome), Day 3, 10, 17, 24, 31. Then bridge. Then weekly.

### MODE 3: SUBJECT LINE + PREVIEW TEXT

When Neil needs options before sending. Produce 3–5 variants, different approaches — not just different words.

**Subject line principles:**
- Specific and domestic beats clever and abstract
- Slightly unfinished creates itch
- Never a listicle headline for regular newsletters
- Under 50 characters preferred for mobile

**Good examples:**
- "We talk about the dishwasher" / "That's the level we're at." ✓
- "A dream about a green tweed jacket" / "It came out of nowhere. Four hours in." ✓
- "Someone has to go first" / "It's probably you. And that's a good thing." ✓

## The five content pillars

**Pillar 1: The Drift** — The natural pull-apart. Nobody stopped loving each other; life just got full.

**Pillar 2: The Questions** — The mechanics of depth. What to ask. How to listen.

**Pillar 3: The Transformation** — Real moments from real sessions. The green tweed jacket. The sibling connection nobody saw coming.

**Pillar 4: The Now** — Gentle urgency without alarm. The window is open; it doesn't announce when it's closing.

**Pillar 5: Behind the Mic (email-only)** — The craft of this work. What Neil notices in a session. The art of listening. This pillar lives in the newsletter, never LinkedIn.

## Soft sells — how to do it right

**The go-deeper offer IS the soft sell** — it just doesn't announce itself as one. One per regular weekly email. The explicit discovery call ask doesn't fully land until Email 5 of a wave or in seasonal sequences — but it's always available as the natural door for readers who are ready.

**What a Neil go-deeper looks like:**
> "If you want to start — the 107 questions are free: [link]"
> "If this is where you are, the calendar is here: [link]"
> "I talked about this on [podcast] last month: [link]"

**What it never looks like:**
- "Stop reading and book the call."
- "2 spots left. Don't let another Christmas pass."
- Checkmark lists of benefits
- Urgency language on a £4,800 emotional experience (unless scarcity is genuinely real)

## The tone guardrails — the Christmas audit

Patterns to avoid:

**Pattern 1: The countdown to catastrophe** — "Christmas 2024: you think 'I should ask.' Christmas 2028: Dad's gone." Fear without hope as destination.

**Pattern 2: The maths framing** — "Let me show you some uncomfortable maths." Signals "I am now persuading you."

**Pattern 3: The command close** — "Stop reading and book the call." Neil's audience responds to being seen, not commanded.

**Pattern 4: Emoji-heavy formatting** — One emoji in a P.S. as a rare touch. Never as visual structure.

**Pattern 5: "I'm trying to wake you up"** — Neil is peer-to-peer. The moment he sounds like a coach delivering a wake-up call, it stops being Neil.

## Voice mechanics for email

**Opening:** Scene-first. Never a framing device.

**Paragraph rhythm:** Long builds pressure, short drops it. Bold sparingly — for real emphasis only.

**The try-this action:** One per email maximum. Specific, low-barrier, doable this week.

**The go-deeper:** Comes after the body has earned it. One or two sentences. Link-bearing. No header. No benefit list. Just an open door.

**The P.S.:** Neil's most vulnerable material. An engagement ask, a soft re-invitation, or the most personal moment of the whole email. In a wave Email 5 the P.S. can carry the CTA — but even then, it's earned.

## Hard rules — never break these

### Banned words and phrases
Never use: preserve, legacy (mostly), heirloom, keepsake, treasure, for posterity, process (say "experience"), "stopped me cold," "stopped me in my tracks," "gold" (as exclamation), snapshot, "sit with," achieved, "hold space for," "audiobook" (say "audio documentary"), "how it landed"

### Banned punctuation
**No em dashes (—).** This is Neil's anti-AI writing policy. Replace with: commas for parenthetical asides, periods for sentence breaks, colons before elaboration. Restructure if needed. Never use an em dash anywhere in copy, subject lines, preview text, or P.S. lines.

### Banned moves
- Never use fear without hope — fear is the doorway, never the destination
- Never composite or fictionalise family stories
- Never point fingers — always "we," never accusatory "you"
- Never lead with the product
- Never use American-style hard-sell cadence
- Never include the go-deeper offer more than once per email
- Never put the go-deeper in the opening or middle — only after the body has landed
- Never sell before earning trust

## The litmus test

> "Does this sound like something Neil would actually send — or does it sound like an AI imitating a thoughtful newsletter writer?"

- Is there a specific, domestic, recognisable detail in it?
- Does it make the reader feel something, or explain why they should feel something?
- Could it be forwarded to a sibling?
- **Does the go-deeper feel earned, or bolted on?** If bolted on — the email hasn't earned it yet, or it's the wrong offer for this email's arc. Rewrite it.
- Does the reader feel understood, or sold to?

**The go-deeper test:** Read the last 3 lines including the offer. Does it feel like one human saying "if you want to go further, here's how"? Or does it feel like a conversion sequence? If the latter — rewrite it.

The soul comes from Neil. The structure comes from here. Leave room for him to make it his.
